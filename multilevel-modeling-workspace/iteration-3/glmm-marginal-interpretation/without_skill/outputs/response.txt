There's one substantive error worth flagging before submission — it's a common one and it matters.

## The Problem: Conditional vs. Marginal OR

The fixed effect from a **logistic GLMM** is a **subject-specific (conditional)** estimate — it is conditioned on the random effects (here, clinic). Your write-up says *"in the population"*, which implies a **population-average (marginal)** interpretation. Those are not the same thing in nonlinear models, and the distinction is not cosmetic.

### Why they diverge in logistic models

In a linear mixed model, conditional and marginal effects are identical (linearity lets you average through the random effects). In a **logistic** model, they are not, because the logit link is nonlinear. When you average the conditional probability curves over the clinic random-effect distribution, the resulting marginal OR is **attenuated toward 1** relative to the conditional OR. Your β = 1.2 / OR = 3.32 lives at the conditional level — it's larger in magnitude than the true population-average OR would be.

### What your coefficient actually means

> *"Within the same clinic, a patient who received the intervention had 3.32 times the odds of adhering compared to a control patient."*

Or equivalently:

> *"Conditional on clinic, the intervention was associated with OR = 3.32 (β = 1.2) for treatment adherence."*

### If you genuinely want a population-average OR

You have two clean paths:

| Approach | What you get |
|---|---|
| **GEE** (generalized estimating equations) | Directly estimates the marginal OR — right tool if the population-average effect is the inferential target |
| **Marginal standardization** from GLMM | Integrate (or simulate) out the random effects from your fitted model to recover marginal predicted probabilities, then compute the marginal OR |

### The rest of the write-up is fine

- exp(1.2) = 3.32 ✓  
- "3.32 times the **odds**" is correct phrasing (not "3.32 times more likely," which would conflate OR with RR) ✓  
- Direction and scale are internally consistent ✓

**Bottom line:** Change "In the population" → "Within a given clinic" (or "Conditional on clinic"), and the sentence accurately reflects what a GLMM fixed effect estimates. If the population-average effect is the scientific question, consider GEE or marginal standardization instead.
