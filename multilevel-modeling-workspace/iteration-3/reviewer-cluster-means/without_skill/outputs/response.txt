This is a genuinely important methodological question, and the reviewer's instinct isn't wrong — but the specific implementation they're suggesting has some real problems worth thinking through before you reanalyze.

## What the reviewer gets right

When the intervention is assigned at the school level, the **school is the effective unit of randomization**. Aggregating to school means does avoid misattributing school-level effects to student-level variation, and it makes the treatment comparison conceptually clean.

## The problem with plain OLS on school means

Unweighted OLS treats all 35 school means as equally reliable observations. They're not — each school mean is an estimate with its own sampling variance, and that variance depends on school size. If your ~30 students/school varies at all, a school with 15 students contributes the same to the regression as one with 50, even though its mean is roughly 1.8× noisier. This leads to inefficient (and potentially biased) estimates of your treatment effect standard error.

## Better options

**Option 1 — Multilevel model (recommended):** A random-intercepts MLM with the school-level intervention as a Level-2 predictor uses all ~1,050 students, properly propagates uncertainty through the nested structure, and lets you include student-level covariates for precision. With 35 schools you're at the low end of common rules of thumb, but the treatment effect estimate and its SE will be well-behaved. The variance component estimates (ICC) will be noisier, but that's not your primary inferential target anyway.

**Option 2 — Weighted OLS on school means:** If you must aggregate (e.g., for simplicity or reviewer expectations), use `lm()` with `weights = n_students` in R, or the equivalent in your software. This down-weights noisy small-school means and gives you something closer to what a proper MLM would produce for the treatment coefficient.

**Option 3 — Unweighted OLS on school means:** Weakest choice. Only defensible if all schools are exactly the same size (then it's equivalent to weighted OLS).

## Practical recommendation

Push back gently on "plain OLS on school means" and propose the MLM instead — or at minimum weighted OLS. The framing you can use with the reviewer: *"Given unequal school sizes, I've used a weighted analysis / MLM to ensure school means are treated with appropriate reliability — this aligns with the school-level focus you described."* That honors their concern (school as unit) while fixing the methodological gap.
