Your current model has two issues worth fixing before you run — and one of them is the most common mistake in stimulus-based perception research. Your advisor's intuition is understandable but the reasoning doesn't hold up under scrutiny.

## Issue 1: Missing random slopes for `condition` by subject

If `condition` varies within subjects — each participant rates images from multiple conditions — then the effect of condition *varies across participants*. Some people are more sensitive to the condition manipulation than others. That between-person variability in the condition effect is part of the data structure, and the correct error term for testing `condition` accounts for it.

Without a random slope, the residual variance is artificially inflated, your SE for `condition` is wrong, and — crucially — the test is **anti-conservative** (inflated Type I error).

```r
# Minimum fix for within-subjects condition
lmer(rating ~ condition + (1 + condition | subject), data = d)
```

## Issue 2: Treating images as fixed when they should be random

This is Clark's (1973) "stimulus-as-fixed-effect fallacy," and it's exactly as relevant to perception images as it is to language stimuli.

The reasoning *"I chose these images, so they're fixed"* conflates **how you selected stimuli** with **the inferential scope of your claims**. The right question is:

> **Do your conclusions apply only to these exact 48 images, or do they generalize to images like these?**

If you're claiming "condition X produces higher ratings" — you mean in general, not just for this specific set of 48. That means images are a **random sample from a population of possible images**, and their variability is a source of uncertainty you need to account for.

If you omit `(1 | image)`:
- Variance between images is dumped into the residual
- The SE for `condition` is understated when condition is a between-image factor (e.g., 24 images per condition)
- Type I error for the condition test is inflated, sometimes dramatically

## The corrected model

```r
library(lme4)
library(lmerTest)  # for Satterthwaite df and p-values

m <- lmer(rating ~ condition + 
            (1 + condition | subject) + 
            (1 | image), 
          data = d)
summary(m)
```

A few things to check after fitting:

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Singular fit warning? | If yes, the random-slope correlation may be over-parameterized; try `(1 + condition \|\| subject)` to remove the correlation |
| Variance for `image`? | Report this — it's the ICC contribution from stimulus variability |
| Variance for `condition` slope? | If near zero and singular, you can simplify, but diagnose first |

## One clarifying question

Does `condition` vary **within** images or **between** images? 

- **Between images** (some images *are* condition A, others are condition B): `(1 | image)` is sufficient for images; no random slope for condition by image is possible
- **Within images** (same image appears in multiple conditions via context manipulation): add `(1 + condition | image)` for the maximal structure

The between-images case is more common in perception experiments and is what I assumed above — but it's worth confirming before you finalize the formula.
