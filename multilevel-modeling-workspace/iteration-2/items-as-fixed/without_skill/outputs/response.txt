## Yes — you're likely missing `(1 | image)`

Your advisor is right that `(1 | subject)` is necessary, but the model as specified has a meaningful gap. Here's the full picture:

---

### The missing random effect: images

Even though you *chose* these 48 images deliberately, they still introduce variance. Some images will consistently get higher ratings than others — that's image-level variance. If you omit `(1 | image)`, that variance doesn't disappear; it collapses into the residual, inflating your effective sample size and making your test of `condition` anti-conservative (spuriously low *p*-values).

This is the **stimuli-as-fixed-effect fallacy** (Clark, 1973; Judd, Westfall & Kenny, 2012) — the same logic that for decades plagued psycholinguistics when researchers treated word items as fixed. The selection mechanism (deliberate vs. random sampling) is separate from whether image-level variance needs to be partitioned out. It does.

**Minimum recommended model:**
```r
lmer(rating ~ condition + (1 | subject) + (1 | image))
```

This is a fully **crossed** random effects design: every subject sees every image, so subject and image are crossed (not nested).

---

### Also consider: a random slope for condition

If `condition` is a property of the images (e.g., images belong to different categories), you should ask: *do participants vary in how strongly they respond to condition?* That's a random slope:

```r
lmer(rating ~ condition + (1 + condition | subject) + (1 | image))
```

Barr et al. (2013) argue for **maximal random effects** justified by the design — if subjects each see all conditions, a by-subject slope for condition is warranted. It costs you some power but protects Type I error rate.

---

### What if condition varies *within* images too?

If each image appears in multiple conditions (e.g., different framing, instructions), you'd also want `(1 + condition | image)`. But if condition is a between-images factor (each image is in exactly one condition), `(1 | image)` alone is correct for the image random effect.

---

### Quick decision guide

| Design feature | Add to model |
|---|---|
| Ratings vary by image | `(1 \| image)` ✅ almost certainly |
| Participants may differ in condition sensitivity | `(condition \| subject)` ✅ if estimable |
| Each image appears in multiple conditions | `(condition \| image)` consider |
