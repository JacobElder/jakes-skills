## Three Critical Problems

### 1. Pseudoreplication (the most serious issue)
Trials are nested within participants, but `lm()` treats all 5,000 as independent. They're not — every trial from the same participant shares that person's baseline ability, response style, and condition assignment. This **deflates standard errors** by roughly a factor of ~3× (assuming a typical ICC of ~0.15 with 50 trials/person), meaning your t-statistics are inflated and your p-values are anti-conservative.

**The effective N for a between-participant interaction (`condition:age`) is ~100, not 5,000.** The 50 trials/person sharpen your estimate of each person's accuracy, but they don't add independent information about who that person is.

### 2. Wrong distributional family
If `accuracy` is trial-level binary (0/1 correct), `lm()` assumes a continuous, unbounded, normally-distributed outcome. A logistic GLMM with `family = binomial` is the correct model. The linear model will produce predicted values outside [0,1] and biased estimates.

### 3. The p = 0.008 interaction cannot be reported
It's derived from wrong degrees of freedom (~4,997 residual df vs. the ~97 that actually apply to a between-participant effect) and severely deflated SEs. The true p-value could easily be > 0.05 once the model is correctly specified.

## The Correct Model

**If accuracy is trial-level binary (recommended):**
```r
library(lme4)

d$age_c <- scale(d$age, center = TRUE, scale = FALSE)  # center age

model <- glmer(
  accuracy ~ condition * age_c + (1 | participant_id),
  data   = d,
  family = binomial(link = "logit"),
  control = glmerControl(optimizer = "bobyqa", optCtrl = list(maxfun = 2e5))
)

summary(model)
isSingular(model)  # check for convergence issues
```
