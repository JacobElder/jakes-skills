## Your interpretation is incorrect — and this is the most consequential mistake in 2×2 MLM write-ups.

### What treatment coding actually gives you

With default treatment coding, **main effect coefficients are simple effects at the reference level of the other factor**, not averages across levels of that factor.

In your model:

| Coefficient | What it actually estimates |
|---|---|
| Intercept | Mean when A = control AND B = low |
| β_A = 0.45 | Effect of A **only when B = low** |
| β_B = 0.12 | Effect of B **only when A = control** |
| β_A×B = 0.55 | How much the effect of A changes from B=low → B=high |

So β_A = 0.45 is the **simple effect of A at B = low**, not the average treatment effect of A across both levels of B.

### What your model actually implies

Given your coefficients:

- **Effect of A when B = low:** 0.45 *(that's your β_A)*
- **Effect of A when B = high:** 0.45 + 0.55 = **1.00**
- **Average effect of A (if cells balanced):** (0.45 + 1.00) / 2 = **0.73**

The number you'd write up as "the treatment effect of A on average" is **0.73, not 0.45** — and the two estimates don't even overlap in a typical confidence interval.

### The fix: use sum or ±0.5 contrast coding

Switch both factors to **sum coding** (or equivalently, ±0.5 deviation coding):

```r
# In R — recode before fitting
contrasts(condition_A) <- c(-0.5, 0.5)  # control = -0.5, treat = +0.5
contrasts(condition_B) <- c(-0.5, 0.5)  # low = -0.5, high = +0.5

model <- lmer(y ~ condition_A * condition_B + (1 + condition_A * condition_B | subject), data = dat)
```

With ±0.5 coding:
- β_A = effect of A **averaged across both levels of B** ← what you wanted
- β_B = effect of B **averaged across both levels of A**
- β_A×B = the interaction (same estimate; the scale changes but it stays interpretable as the crossover in cell means)

### One more problem: you have a significant interaction

Even after re-coding, reporting a main effect of A without qualification is misleading when the interaction is significant. A significant interaction means **the effect of A depends on the level of B** — so the average is real but potentially uninterpretable as a single number. The write-up should:

1. Report the main effect of A (with proper coding) as a qualified summary
2. Then decompose it into simple effects of A at each level of B
3. Explicitly acknowledge that the interaction means these simple effects differ

### Recommended write-up structure

> *Condition A and B were coded with ±0.5 deviation contrasts. The main effect of A (β = 0.73, SE = …, p = …) was qualified by a significant A × B interaction (β = 0.55, …). Simple effects revealed that the effect of A was smaller at low B (β = 0.45, …) than at high B (β = 1.00, …).*

---

**Bottom line:** Re-fit with sum/deviation coding before writing anything up. The sentence you drafted — "the treatment effect of condition A was significant on average (β = 0.45)" — is incorrect under treatment coding (0.45 is a simple effect, not an average) and would be an interpretive error in a manuscript.
