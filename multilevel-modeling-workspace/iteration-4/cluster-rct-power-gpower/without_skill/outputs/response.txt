The approach has a legitimate foundation, but there are several issues that need to be resolved before this is grant-ready. Let me walk through what's right, what's incomplete, and what's potentially wrong.

---

## What's Actually Valid

Analyzing 20 school means per arm with a two-sample t-test is a legitimate "summary statistics" approach for cluster RCTs. When cluster sizes are equal, it's approximately equivalent to a linear mixed model and is well-supported in the methodology literature (Murray 1998; Donner & Klar 2000). The unit of randomization (school) is the unit of analysis — that part is correct.

---

## Critical Gap: The Effect Size Almost Certainly Needs Conversion

This is the most likely error, and it's not minor.

G*Power is treating schools as your units. The Cohen's *d* you entered should be based on the **standard deviation of school means**, not student-level SD. These are not the same:

$$\sigma_{\bar{y}} = \sqrt{\sigma^2_b + \frac{\sigma^2_w}{n_{students}}} = \sigma_{student}\sqrt{\text{ICC} + \frac{1 - \text{ICC}}{n}}$$

The effect size at the school level is:

$$d_{school} = \frac{\Delta}{\sigma_{\bar{y}}} = \frac{d_{student}}{\sqrt{\text{ICC} + \dfrac{1-\text{ICC}}{n}}}$$

**Concrete example.** Suppose you want to detect a student-level effect of $d_{student} = 0.30$ and you assume ICC = 0.10, $n = 25$:

$$\sqrt{0.10 + \frac{0.90}{25}} = \sqrt{0.136} \approx 0.369$$

$$d_{school} = \frac{0.30}{0.369} \approx 0.81$$

If you entered $d = 0.30$ into G*Power and treated schools as units, your power estimate is for detecting a much smaller effect than you'd actually expect — meaning your true power is *higher* than 82%, but more importantly: **your stated effect size target in the proposal is wrong**. Reviewers will ask what effect size you're powering for, and "0.30 at the school level" is a very different (and implausibly large) claim than "0.30 at the student level."

**What to do:** Specify the student-level effect size you consider the minimum clinically/practically meaningful difference, obtain or estimate an ICC from a pilot or the literature, compute $d_{school}$, and re-enter that into G*Power.

---

## The ICC Is Not Stated Anywhere

Even with the school-level analysis, grant reviewers and study section statisticians will expect:

- An explicit ICC assumption (education outcomes typically range 0.05–0.20 for achievement)
- A citation or pilot data justifying it
- A sensitivity check showing power under a range of ICC values (e.g., ICC = 0.05, 0.10, 0.20)

Without this, the proposal looks like the clustering was ignored rather than handled.

---

## Moderate Issue: Variable Cluster Sizes

"About 25 students per school" means unequal cluster sizes. The t-test on school means assumes equal $n_k$. With variable sizes:

- Unweighted school means give each school equal weight regardless of size, which is inefficient and potentially biased if the ICC is nonzero
- The correct approach is **precision-weighted analysis** or, better, a **mixed model** which handles unequal cluster sizes naturally

For a grant proposal, you should either (a) justify that cluster sizes are sufficiently similar that the simple means approach is adequate, or (b) specify mixed models as the primary analysis and compute power accordingly.

---

## Alignment With Planned Analysis

Power calculations should match the analysis you'll actually run. If your SAP will use a linear mixed model (students nested in schools, treatment at school level), the power analysis should be based on that model — not a t-test that may differ in efficiency. Misalignment between the power analysis and the statistical analysis plan is a common reviewer flag.

---

## What Needs to Happen Before Submission

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| **1** | Decide the minimum meaningful student-level effect size ($\Delta$ and $\sigma_{student}$) |
| **2** | Obtain or cite an ICC estimate; run sensitivity over plausible range |
| **3** | Convert to $d_{school}$ using the formula above |
| **4** | Re-run G*Power (or use `clusterPower` / `PowerUpR` in R for more flexibility) with the correct effect size |
| **5** | Check that the power analysis method matches your planned analysis |
| **6** | Report: $n_{schools}$, $n_{students/school}$, assumed ICC, $d_{student}$, $d_{school}$, $\alpha$, power |

The committee sign-off is not a substitute for methodological completeness — study section reviewers often include cluster RCT specialists who will check exactly these things. Getting this right before submission is much easier than responding to a scored critique.
