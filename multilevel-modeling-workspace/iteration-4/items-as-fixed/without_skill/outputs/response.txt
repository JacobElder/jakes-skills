Your advisor is right that a random intercept for subject is necessary — but **there's a critical missing piece**: a random intercept for image.

## The crossed random-effects structure

Your design is **fully crossed**: 60 subjects × 48 images, every subject sees every image. That gives you two independent sources of non-independence that both need to be in the model:

```r
lmer(rating ~ condition + (1 | subject) + (1 | image))
```

Without `(1 | image)`, the variance due to images (some images are just rated higher than others) gets absorbed into the residual — and that inflates your Type I error rate for the condition test. This is Clark's (1973) "language-as-fixed-effect fallacy" generalized to visual stimuli, and it's directly addressed by Judd, Westfall & Kenny (2012).

## The "but my images are fixed/selected" objection

This is where your advisor's reasoning needs one more step. The argument "images aren't random, so no random effect" conflates two different questions:

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Were images randomly sampled from a population? | No — you selected them |
| Do images vary in their baseline rating, and do I want to generalize beyond these 48? | Almost certainly yes |

Even if your 48 images are a purposive set, they still differ in mean rating — that variance needs to be partitioned out. And you almost certainly want the condition effect to generalize beyond *exactly these 48 images*. Without `(1 | image)`, you're not testing that.

## Before you run: one critical question

**How is `condition` assigned?** This changes the random slopes you need:

**Condition is a property of the image** (e.g., 24 images are emotionally positive, 24 negative):
```r
# Minimum defensible model — condition varies only between images,
# so no random slope for condition by subject is estimable
lmer(rating ~ condition + (1 | subject) + (1 | image))
```
Be aware: with only 48 images, you're testing condition with 48 image-level units. That's modest power.

**Condition varies within subjects AND within images** (e.g., subjects see each image twice under different instructions):
```r
# Maximal model — condition varies within both subjects and images
lmer(rating ~ condition + (condition | subject) + (condition | image))
```
Start maximal and simplify only if you hit convergence problems.

## What to check after fitting

- **Singular fit warning?** Check if the image variance component collapsed to zero. If so, your 48 images are surprisingly homogeneous — worth knowing, but still report it.
- **ICC for image**: How much of the total variance is between images? This is the quantity that quantifies how badly omitting `(1 | image)` would have hurt you.
- **Compare the condition *p*-value** between your original model and the crossed model — if it changes substantially, that's the inflated-error problem made visible.

The bottom line: `(1 | subject) + (1 | image)` is the minimum for a fully-crossed subjects-by-stimuli design. The single random intercept your advisor approved is a common starting point but misses half the crossed structure.
