## No — that interpretation is incorrect, and the error is consequential here.

### What treatment coding actually gives you

With treatment (dummy) coding in a 2×2 design, **main effects are not average effects — they are conditional effects at the reference level of the other factor.**

| Coefficient | What it actually estimates |
|---|---|
| Intercept | Mean when A = control *and* B = low |
| β_A = 0.45 | Effect of A **only when B = low** |
| β_B = 0.12 | Effect of B **only when A = control** |
| β_A×B = 0.55 | How much the effect of A *changes* as B goes low → high |

So β_A = 0.45 is not "the treatment effect of A on average" — it's **the simple effect of A at B = low**.

### Why your significant interaction makes this critical

The effect of A at B = high is:

```
β_A + β_A×B = 0.45 + 0.55 = 1.00
```

The true *average* effect of A (marginal across both levels of B) is:

```
(0.45 + 1.00) / 2 = 0.725
```

You're writing up 0.45 as the average effect when the actual average effect is 0.725 — a 61% understatement — and the effect at B = high is more than double the effect at B = low. **Treatment coding + a significant interaction makes this the worst possible case for misreading main effects as averages.**

---

### Two clean fixes

#### Fix 1 — Recode with sum / ±0.5 contrasts (recommended)

With sum coding, main effects genuinely represent the marginal mean collapsed across the other factor.

```r
# In R — apply before fitting
contrasts(df$A) <- c(-0.5, 0.5)   # control = -0.5, treat = +0.5
contrasts(df$B) <- c(-0.5, 0.5)   # low = -0.5, high = +0.5
```

Now β_A *is* the average effect of A across both levels of B, and your interpretation would be correct. This is the right choice for a confirmatory 2×2 where you want to test main effects and their interaction cleanly.

#### Fix 2 — Use emmeans to get simple effects (if you keep treatment coding)

```r
library(emmeans)
emm <- emmeans(model, ~ A | B)   # effect of A at each level of B
contrast(emm, "pairwise")
```

This gives you:
- Effect of A when B = low (= 0.45 from your model)
- Effect of A when B = high (= 1.00)
- The marginal effect of A collapsed across B

---

### What the write-up should say (with your current treatment-coded model)

> "The effect of condition A was significant when condition B was at its low level (β = 0.45, p = .02). A significant interaction (β = 0.55, p = .01) indicated that the effect of A was substantially larger when B was high (simple effect = 1.00) than when B was low (simple effect = 0.45). [Report marginal effect if desired.]"

---

### Bottom line

The lme4 default of treatment coding is a poor choice for interaction models precisely because of this trap. For any 2×2 where you care about main effects, refit with ±0.5 contrasts — it makes main effects interpretable as averages, keeps the interaction coefficient identical in meaning, and eliminates this class of write-up error entirely.
