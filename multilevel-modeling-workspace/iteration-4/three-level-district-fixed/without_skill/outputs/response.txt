This proposal has a **fatal structural flaw** that would make the model unestimable — and a secondary misspecification even if that flaw were somehow patched. Let me walk through both.

---

## Problem 1: Perfect Collinearity — The Policy Effect Is Not Identified

The policy variable is a **district-level constant**: every student in district *d* has the same value (0 or 1). The 14 district dummies are a complete encoding of district membership. Therefore:

> **policy = a linear combination of the district dummies**

If you know which district a student belongs to (which the dummies tell you completely), you know their policy status with certainty. The model matrix is rank-deficient. There is no remaining between-district variation with which to estimate the policy coefficient.

`lmer` will detect this and either silently return `NA` for the policy coefficient or emit a rank-deficiency warning and drop a term. It won't give you a false answer — it simply **cannot give you any answer**.

The intuition your collaborator is reaching for — "fixed effects control for confounders" — is correct but misapplied. Fixed effects at level *d* absorb **all** between-level-*d* variation. That works beautifully when the treatment varies *within* level *d* (e.g., a within-person treatment with person fixed effects). Here the treatment **only** varies between districts, so district FEs don't just remove confounders; they remove the signal entirely.

---

## Problem 2: The Nesting Structure Is Misspecified

Even setting aside collinearity, `(1 | school)` alone is wrong for this data:

| Level | Clustering unit | Model accounts for it? |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Districts | ❌ No district random effect |
| 2 | Schools within districts | ✅ School random intercept |
| 1 | Students within schools | ✅ (residual) |

Omitting the district random effect means:
- School-level residuals within the same district are **not independent** (they share a district-level shock), but the model treats them as if they are
- Standard errors for the policy effect will be **anti-conservative** — you're ignoring the most relevant level of clustering for the variable you're testing
- The ICC at the district level is simply ignored

---

## The Correct Model

Your data are three-level: students nested in schools nested in districts. Policy is a level-3 predictor. The appropriate structure is:

```r
lmer(score ~ policy + district_covariates + (1 | district/school),
     data = df, REML = TRUE)
```

which expands to `(1 | district) + (1 | school:district)`.

This model:
- Estimates the policy effect from **between-district comparisons** (treated vs. control districts), exactly as the design intends
- Accounts for district-level clustering via a district random intercept (partial pooling, not absorbing)
- Accounts for school-level clustering nested within districts
- Allows district-level covariates (urbanicity, baseline SES, prior test scores, etc.) to soak up confounders **without** eliminating the policy signal

---

## A Necessary Warning on Power

With 15 districts and a district-level treatment, the **effective sample size for the policy test is ~13 degrees of freedom** (15 districts, minus the intercept and policy coefficient) — not 600. The 600 students buy power for student-level questions; they do not help you here.

Satterthwaite or Kenward-Roger df in `lmerTest` will reflect this:

```r
library(lmerTest)
m <- lmer(score ~ policy + district_covariates + (1 | district/school), data = df)
summary(m)  # df for policy will be ~13, not 598
```

With 7–8 districts per arm, you have limited power to detect anything but large policy effects, and variance component estimates at the district level will be noisy. If you're committed to frequentist inference, report this honestly. If you have flexibility, Bayesian estimation with weakly informative priors (via `brms`) will regularize the district-level variance component more stably than REML with so few clusters.

---

## Summary

| Issue | Proposed model | Correct model |
|---|---|---|
| **Collinearity** | Policy ≡ linear combo of district dummies → NA coefficient | Policy is a free predictor; district random effects allow identification |
| **Nesting** | `(1 \| school)` only — ignores district clustering | `(1 \| district/school)` — both levels |
| **Inference df** | Implicitly ~598 | ~13 (Satterthwaite will show this) |
| **Confounder control** | Intended but self-defeating | District-level covariates in the fixed part |

The collaborator's goal — controlling for district-level confounders — is correct and important. The mechanism for achieving it is district-level **covariates** in the fixed part, not district **fixed effects**, which are the wrong tool whenever your treatment is itself a district-level variable.
