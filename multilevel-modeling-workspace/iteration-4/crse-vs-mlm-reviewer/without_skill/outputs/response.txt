## Why Your Approach Is Correct

### 1. Two-way FE + clustered SEs *is* the multilevel solution for your problem

The reviewer likely understands "multilevel model" to mean a mixed-effects model with firm random effects (e.g., `mixed` in Stata or `lmer` in R). But your estimator already handles the two things multilevel models are meant to address:

| Concern | What Reviewer Expects | What You're Already Doing |
|---|---|---|
| Within-firm correlation across years | Firm random effects | Firm fixed effects + clustered SEs |
| Shared time shocks | Year random effects | Year fixed effects |
| Heteroskedasticity / serial correlation | Modeled via VC structure | `vce(cluster firmid)` |

Clustering at the firm level gives SEs that are robust to *any* arbitrary within-firm correlation structure across time — more general, not less, than a parametric multilevel model.

### 2. Fixed effects are strictly preferable when treatment is potentially endogenous

This is the decisive point for economics. A multilevel (random effects) model requires:

> **E[αᵢ | Xᵢₜ] = 0** — firm-specific effects are uncorrelated with all regressors

In firm-level productivity research, this assumption is almost never credible. Firms that select into treatment almost certainly differ systematically on unobservables (management quality, capital structure, unobserved shocks). Fixed effects are consistent under treatment endogeneity; random effects are **biased**.

This is why the Hausman test exists — and in economics, firm FE almost always wins.

### 3. Your design has no small-cluster problem

Clustering asymptotics require large *N* in the clustering dimension. You have 800 firms — that's well above the ~50-cluster threshold where cluster-robust inference is reliable. No issue here.

## Where Reviewer #2 May Be Coming From (and Why It Doesn't Apply)

Reviewers from psychology, sociology, or education research are trained that "ignoring nested structure" = inflated Type I error. They're right in their context: OLS *without* clustering on nested data underestimates SEs. But you're not doing plain OLS — you're clustering. That correction is already there.

The reviewer is essentially saying "you need a multilevel model" when you've already implemented the econometrically superior version of one.

---

## One Legitimate Concern to Rule Out

The one area where TWFE genuinely *can* fail — and where the reviewer's concern might have a kernel of truth — is **staggered treatment adoption with heterogeneous treatment effects**. If different firms entered treatment at different times, Goodman-Bacon (2021) and Callaway & Sant'Anna (2021) show TWFE can produce negatively-weighted ATTs. 

Ask yourself: Is treatment timing staggered across firms? If yes, this is worth addressing (via `csdid` or `eventstudyinteract`), but the fix is a better DiD estimator — not a multilevel model.
