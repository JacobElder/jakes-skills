# Barrier library: behavioral phenomena that break the path

A reference catalog of the behavioral phenomena that most often create bottlenecks, with how
each tends to *show up* in a context and what tends to *address* it. 

**Read this the right way.** This is a generator and a naming aid for hypotheses formed during
Diagnosis — it is not a checklist to file barriers under, and it is not a place to shop for a
single bias to blame. A behavior gap usually involves several of these at once; the same
phenomenon appears at different steps; and a label is worth nothing until evidence backs it.
Pair every entry you invoke with the specific *contextual feature* producing it and the
*evidence* that would confirm or kill it (see `diagnose.md`).

## Attention & memory

- **Limited attention / inattention** — The choice or cue never registers; banner blindness,
  email overload, competing priorities. *Shows up as:* people who "would have" acted but never
  noticed the moment. *Addressed by:* salience, timing the prompt to the moment of action,
  removing competing noise.
- **Forgetting** — Intention formed, action missed because it wasn't top-of-mind in the action
  window. The dominant *action* barrier. *Shows up as:* high intention, low follow-through;
  worse with long intention→action delays and narrow windows. *Addressed by:* reminders
  (often beats incentives), reducing the delay, widening the window.
- **Salience** — Relevant information or options aren't noticed because they don't stand out.
  *Addressed by:* making the key option/figure visually or temporally prominent.

## Friction & effort

- **Hassle factors** — Small roadblocks (an extra form field, a login, a stamp, an ambiguous
  step) with wildly outsized effects. The signature ideas42 concept: a 100-question FAFSA
  suppresses thousands of dollars of aid uptake. *Shows up as:* drop-off concentrated at a
  specific friction point. *Addressed by:* removing/consolidating steps, pre-filling,
  autofill, providing hands-on assistance.
- **Tunneling / scarcity** — Under chronic scarcity (money, time, bandwidth), attention tunnels
  onto the urgent and crowds out the important; cognitive bandwidth is literally taxed. *Shows
  up as:* low-income/time-poor/high-stress people missing beneficial actions that look "easy"
  from outside. *Addressed by:* cutting costs (steps), creating slack, timing asks away from
  peak stress (see scarcity design principles in `assess-and-scale.md`).
- **Planning fallacy** — Underestimating how long an action takes, so it's deferred or
  under-resourced. *Addressed by:* implementation prompts, realistic time framing, defaults.

## Choice architecture

- **Default bias / inertia / status-quo bias** — Whatever happens with no active choice tends
  to win, via social proof, status-quo preference, and procrastination. The 401(k)
  auto-enrollment jump (37%→85%) and opt-out organ donation (12%→99%) are the canonical results.
  *Addressed by:* setting the welfare-improving option as the default (where ethical and legal).
- **Choice overload / choice aversion** — Too many or too-complex options demotivate choice or
  push people to bad simplifying heuristics (health-plan paralysis). *Addressed by:* reducing or
  grouping options, smart defaults, recommendation engines, personalized shortlists.
- **Primacy / frequency / mere exposure** — Options seen first or more often are likelier to be
  chosen, even without awareness. *Shows up as:* order and repetition effects in a choice set.
- **Framing & prospect theory** — The same option is weighted differently as a gain vs. a loss;
  **loss aversion** makes a loss feel ≈2× a same-size gain; the **endowment effect** inflates
  the value of what one already holds. *Addressed by:* loss-framing where appropriate (the
  loss-framed teacher-bonus result), careful gain/loss framing of consequences.
- **Present bias / hyperbolic discounting** — Immediate costs and rewards dominate larger
  future ones (saving, exercise, training, preventive health). *Addressed by:* making future
  consequences salient now, immediate rewards, commitment devices, Save-More-Tomorrow-style
  pre-commitment.

## Social & identity

- **Social norms / social proof** — People do what they believe similar others do; perceived
  norms can help or hurt. *Caution:* publicizing an undesirable behavior as common can normalize
  it (the dropout-campaign backfire). *Addressed by:* truthful norm messaging, peer comparison,
  making desirable behavior visibly common.
- **Identity & social meaning** — Behavior that conflicts with self-image is resisted; behavior
  that affirms an aspired identity is adopted. *Addressed by:* framing the action in terms of an
  identity the person embraces.
- **Social influence / reciprocity / commitment-to-others** — Promises and visibility to others
  raise follow-through. *Addressed by:* public commitments, accountability partners.

## Beliefs & understanding (handle with care)

- **Mental models** — People act on an internal story of how things work; a wrong-but-coherent
  model (clear water = safe water) blocks the target behavior. *Addressed by:* experiences that
  update the model, not just facts thrown at it.
- **Availability bias** — Vivid, recently-heard cases dominate judgment (overheard fears about
  chlorine). *Addressed by:* countering with vivid, trustworthy, locally-relevant information.
- **Genuine information gaps** — Sometimes the person truly lacks a key fact. *But beware:* this
  is the barrier amateurs over-diagnose because it licenses the easy intervention (a pamphlet, a
  training). Information alone rarely changes behavior; demand evidence before betting on it, and
  remember that warning-label and risk-info programs frequently fail or backfire.

## Self-control & follow-through

- **Procrastination / deferral** — Putting off an unpleasant-feeling action; tiny barriers
  derail completion. *Addressed by:* reducing startup friction, deadlines, default scheduling.
- **Sunk-cost effects** — Prior investment raises follow-through (can be harnessed positively,
  e.g., prepaid membership) but also causes irrational persistence. *Addressed by:* designing
  in early constructive commitment.
- **Intention–action gap** — The umbrella phenomenon: deciding ≠ doing. Most action-stage
  barriers above are flavors of it. *Addressed by:* implementation intentions, reminders,
  commitments, friction reduction at the action moment.

## How to use an entry

When a lens in `diagnose.md` surfaces a candidate barrier, find it here to (1) name it
precisely, (2) borrow the contextual features it usually rides on so you can check your own
case, and (3) preview the intervention families that tend to address it — which you'll expand in
`engineer.md`. Always carry forward the *contextual feature* and the *evidence*, never the bare
label.
