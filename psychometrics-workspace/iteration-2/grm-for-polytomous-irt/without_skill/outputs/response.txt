Great question! The 2PL (two-parameter logistic) model is a popular choice in IRT and could be a reasonable starting point for your analysis, though there are a few things worth considering given your data structure.

The 2PL model estimates two parameters per item: difficulty (how hard or extreme the item is) and discrimination (how well the item differentiates between respondents at different trait levels). It's widely used and well-understood, which makes it a solid foundation for IRT work.

That said, since your items use a 5-point scale rather than a simple yes/no format, you might want to think about whether 2PL fully captures your data. Some researchers using Likert-type scales will dichotomize their responses (e.g., collapsing "never/rarely" vs. "sometimes/often/always") to use binary IRT models like 2PL. This can work reasonably well in practice, especially if you're primarily interested in whether respondents endorse burnout at all.

However, if you want to make full use of the ordinal information in your 5-point responses, you might also consider polytomous IRT models like the Graded Response Model (GRM) or the Generalized Partial Credit Model (GPCM), which are designed for ordered response categories. These models are also available in R — for example, the `mirt` package supports a range of IRT models. GRM and GPCM will typically give you richer item-level information since they model each response category separately.

In short: 2PL is a good starting point and many researchers use it even with Likert data after some recoding. If you want to preserve the full response scale, GRM or GPCM would be the more technically appropriate choice. It depends on your research goals and how much complexity you want to manage.
