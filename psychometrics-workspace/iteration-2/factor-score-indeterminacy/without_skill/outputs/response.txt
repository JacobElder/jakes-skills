Yes, that's a standard and reasonable workflow for using EFA results in further analyses.

After running `psych::fa()`, the `$scores` component contains estimated factor scores for each observation — one score per factor per participant. These are computed using regression-based estimation by default (Thurstone scores), which predicts each person's factor score from their item responses weighted by the factor loadings.

Using those scores as predictors in multiple regression is a common approach and has a few practical advantages:

- **Dimensionality reduction**: instead of entering all your individual items as predictors (which can cause multicollinearity and overfitting), you're entering a smaller set of factor scores that capture the underlying latent structure.
- **Interpretability**: regression coefficients for factor scores are easier to interpret than coefficients for individual correlated items.
- **Handling multicollinearity**: if your items are highly correlated, factor scores can serve as orthogonalized (or at least less correlated) predictors.

A few practical notes:

1. **Check for oblique vs. orthogonal rotation**: if you used an oblique rotation (e.g., `rotate = "oblimin"`), your factor scores may themselves be correlated, which partially reintroduces collinearity. You might consider Anderson-Rubin scores if you want orthogonal predictors: `psych::fa(..., scores = "Anderson")`.

2. **Missing data**: factor scores will be `NA` for participants with missing item responses unless you've handled missingness beforehand.

3. **Overfitting concern**: since the factor structure was derived from the same dataset you're using for regression, there's some risk of capitalizing on chance. Cross-validation or a holdout sample helps.

4. **Reporting**: in your methods section, note the extraction method, rotation, and number of factors, and specify that factor scores were used as predictors rather than individual items.

Overall, `fa()$scores` → regression is a well-established workflow. Just document your choices clearly.
