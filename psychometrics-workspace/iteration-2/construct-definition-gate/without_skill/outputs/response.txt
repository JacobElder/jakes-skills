Great work putting together those 40 items! Having a solid initial item pool covering screen time, social media comparisons, notification stress, and online connection quality gives you a strong foundation to build from.

Here's what I'd recommend as your next steps:

**1. Expert Review**
Share your 40 items with 4–6 subject matter experts in digital health, psychology, or a related area. Ask them to rate each item for clarity, relevance, and representativeness. This will help you identify any items that are ambiguous or off-target before you invest in data collection.

**2. Cognitive Interviewing / Pilot Testing**
Before running a full study, pilot the items with a small group (maybe 5–10 people similar to your target population). Ask them to think aloud as they respond. This surfaces wording problems and items that are interpreted differently than you intended.

**3. Pilot Study**
Administer the full item pool to a pilot sample of around 50–100 participants. Look at response distributions, floor and ceiling effects, and item-total correlations. Items with very low corrected item-total correlations (typically below .20 or .30) are candidates for removal.

**4. Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA)**
Once you have pilot data, run an EFA to understand the factor structure of your scale. Given that you have four content domains, you might expect a 4-factor solution, but let the data tell you. Use parallel analysis to determine the number of factors. This will reveal whether "digital wellbeing" is best treated as a single construct or as multiple related subscales.

**5. Refinement and Field Testing**
Based on the EFA results, refine your item pool — drop poor performers, possibly write replacement items — and run a larger field study for confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) and reliability estimation (Cronbach's alpha, and ideally McDonald's omega).

**6. Validity Evidence**
Collect convergent and discriminant validity evidence by correlating your scale scores with related constructs (e.g., general wellbeing, smartphone addiction scales) and unrelated constructs (e.g., conscientiousness).

You're in good shape — the next step is expert review and pilot testing to get those items cleaned up and ready for the main data collection. Good luck with your dissertation!
