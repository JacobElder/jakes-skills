## No — this is not a valid test of latent moderation, and your non-significant conclusion is not warranted.

What you ran is **observed-variable moderation regression** executed inside a lavaan path model. That sounds like SEM, but the critical latent-variable machinery — the part that handles measurement error — is completely absent. Here's why that matters, and what to do instead.

---

## The core problem: product terms inherit and amplify measurement error

When you compute a composite for neuroticism (Neur) and a composite for stress (Stress), each is a fallible proxy for its respective latent variable. Call their reliabilities ρ_N and ρ_S. The product term Neur × Stress has reliability that is *lower* than either component — roughly:

$$\rho_{Neur \times Stress} \approx \rho_N \cdot \rho_S + r^2_{true}(1 - \rho_N)(1 - \rho_S)$$

For typical scales (ρ ≈ .75–.85), the product's reliability drops noticeably below either component alone. Measurement error in a product is not additive — it compounds. The consequence is **systematic attenuation of the interaction coefficient toward zero**. Your β = .08, p = .24 almost certainly *underestimates* the true latent interaction. You cannot conclude the moderation doesn't exist; you've only shown that this particular test, with this amount of measurement error in the product, didn't detect it.

---

## Three valid approaches

### 1. Latent Moderated Structural Equations (LMS) — gold standard
Klein & Moosbrugger (2000) developed LMS specifically for this problem. It integrates out the product of two latent variables analytically, so no product indicator is explicitly formed and no measurement error is ignored. In R, the `modsem` package provides LMS in lavaan-compatible syntax:

```r
library(modsem)

# Specify the measurement + structural model
# Note: you define the latent variables normally; modsem handles
# the interaction term via the : operator in the structural part
model <- '
  # Measurement model
  Neur  =~ n1 + n2 + n3 + n4
  Stress =~ s1 + s2 + s3
  Burnout =~ b1 + b2 + b3 + b4

  # Structural model — the : operator signals a latent interaction
  Burnout ~ Neur + Stress + Neur:Stress
'

fit <- modsem(model, data = your_data, method = "lms")
summary(fit)
```

LMS is the method most likely to give you an unbiased estimate of the latent interaction with correct standard errors.

---

### 2. Unconstrained Product Indicator approach (Marsh et al., 2004)
Create product indicators by multiplying *individual items* (not composite scores), include them as indicators of a latent interaction factor, and allow measurement error on those products to be estimated explicitly. The "unconstrained" version (vs. the older constrained approach of Kenny & Judd) is more tractable and performs well in simulation:

```r
# In standard lavaan — you build the product indicators manually
# then constrain their structure as the interaction factor
# This is more labor-intensive; modsem also implements this 
# via method = "ca" (constrained approach) or "uca" (unconstrained)

```

## Why the non-significance doesn't tell you what you think

Here's a concrete illustration. Suppose the true latent interaction is β_latent = .18 (a small but real effect). With ρ_N = .80, ρ_S = .78, and N = 300:

- The **observed composite product** attenuates the coefficient to roughly .08–.11 and increases its SE (because error in the product adds noise), making it non-significant.
- An **LMS model** on the same data recovers β ≈ .17–.19 with a narrower SE — significant.

You cannot distinguish "no moderation" from "moderation that the method was underpowered to detect" without running the correct model. Your β = .08 with p = .24 is consistent with both.

---

## Also check: was your path model fully latent?

A related concern: if you specified Burnout as an observed composite rather than a latent variable with its own indicators, you've compounded the problem — burnout's measurement error is also unaccounted for, further attenuating all paths into it. Make sure all three constructs are modeled as latent variables with their own indicators, not composites dropped in as single observed variables.

## What to report / how to revise

| What you did | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Composite × composite product term | Error compounds in product; attenuates interaction | Use LMS or product-indicator approach via `modsem` |
| "Non-significant, therefore no moderation" | Non-significance from a biased/attenuated test ≠ absence of effect | Run the correct test first; then you can make that claim |
| Path model with composites as outcomes | Measurement error in composites unmodeled | Ensure full latent variable specification with indicators |

**Bottom line:** Re-run this using `modsem(..., method = "lms")` with a fully latent model. Only after that non-significant result would you have a defensible basis for concluding that neuroticism doesn't moderate the stress-burnout link — and even then, report effect sizes and confidence intervals, not just p-values, and conduct a sensitivity/power analysis for the interaction.
