## Your co-author is only partially right — and the margins here should make you cautious

You've correctly applied the Fornell-Larcker (FL) criterion, and you do technically pass it. But "technically passes FL" and "adequate evidence for discriminant validity" are meaningfully different claims. Here's why the distinction matters for your specific values.

---

### The core problem with the Fornell-Larcker criterion

Henseler, Ringle, & Sarstedt (2015, *JAMS*) demonstrated via simulation that the FL criterion has **poor sensitivity** — it routinely fails to flag discriminant validity problems, especially when factor correlations are moderate-to-high. It was derived in 1981 for a different SEM context and is now widely considered a **necessary but insufficient** condition.

In plain terms: passing FL tells you the situation isn't catastrophically bad, not that discriminant validity is established.

---

### Your specific numbers are a red flag, not a green light

Work through what these values actually represent:

| | Value |
|---|---|
| AVE (exhaustion) | .54 |
| AVE (depersonalization) | .51 |
| Squared factor correlation (φ²) | .46 |
| Implied factor correlation (φ) | **.68** |
| FL margin for exhaustion | .54 − .46 = **.08** |
| FL margin for depersonalization | .51 − .46 = **.05** |

Two things stand out:

1. **The factor correlation is .68.** These two constructs share 46% of their variance with each other — nearly as much as each shares with its own indicators. For burnout subdimensions (Maslach's MBI), some correlation is expected, but this level invites scrutiny.
2. **The margins are razor-thin.** An FL margin of .05 means that any reasonable sampling variability, alternative indicator set, or slightly different scoring could flip the result. You're not comfortably above the threshold; you're barely clearing it.

---

### What you should also report (or check before claiming victory)

**1. The HTMT ratio (Heterotrait-Monotrait)**
This is the current best-practice criterion (Henseler et al., 2015). It compares the average hetero-trait/hetero-method correlations to the geometric mean of the average mono-trait/hetero-method correlations. Thresholds:
- HTMT < .85 (strict; conceptually distinct constructs)
- HTMT < .90 (liberal; conceptually related constructs)

In lavaan, you can extract this manually from the correlation matrix, or use the `semTools` package:
```r
library(semTools)
htmt(model, data = yourdata)
```
Given your factor correlation of .68, your HTMT is likely elevated. If it exceeds .85 (or approaches .90), your co-author's confidence is not warranted.

**2. Chi-square difference test (model comparison)**
Fit a constrained model where φ is fixed to 1.0, and compare it to your freely estimated model:
```r
# Free model (your current model)
fit_free <- cfa(model, data = yourdata)

# Constrained model
model_constrained <- '...' # same model, with factor correlation fixed to 1
fit_constrained <- cfa(model_constrained, data = yourdata, 
                        orthogonal = FALSE)

# Test
lavTestLRT(fit_free, fit_constrained)
```
A significant Δχ² indicates the factors are statistically distinguishable. This is a direct test the FL criterion never performs.

**3. HTMT confidence interval (bootstrap)**
Bootstrap a 95% CI around the HTMT ratio. If the CI excludes 1.0, that's positive evidence. If it doesn't, you have a problem regardless of FL.
