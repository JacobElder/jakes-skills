The Fornell-Larcker criterion *is* satisfied by the numbers. But the current SEM literature has largely moved past treating it as sufficient evidence of discriminant validity. Here's a precise breakdown of why, and what you should actually do.

### What the Fornell-Larcker Criterion Actually Tests

Fornell & Larcker (1981) operationalized discriminant validity as: each construct's AVE should exceed the *squared* inter-construct correlation (i.e., the shared variance between factors should be less than each factor's average shared variance with its own indicators). Your numbers pass:

| Construct | AVE | Squared *r* | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exhaustion | .54 | .46 | +.08 |
| Depersonalization | .51 | .46 | **+.05** |

Those margins are thin — especially for depersonalization. A margin of .05 is functionally trivial, and given sampling variability in your loadings and factor correlation, this likely wouldn't hold under small perturbations to the data.

---

### Why the Criterion Is No Longer Considered Sufficient

Henseler, Ringle, & Sarstedt (2015, *JAMS*) ran an extensive simulation study demonstrating that the Fornell-Larcker criterion has **poor sensitivity** — it fails to detect discriminant validity problems even when constructs are empirically indistinguishable, particularly when factor loadings are roughly equal across constructs (which they often are when scales are carefully developed). This paper has become a methodological benchmark, and reviewers at top journals (e.g., *JPSP*, *OBHDP*, *JAP*) now routinely expect something beyond Fornell-Larcker.

---

### What You Should Report Instead: HTMT

The **Heterotrait-Monotrait ratio of correlations (HTMT)** is now the preferred discriminant validity criterion. It's the ratio of the average heteromethod-heterotrait correlations to the average heteromethod-monotrait correlations. Thresholds:

- **HTMT < .85** — conservative criterion (Kline, 2016; conceptually distinct constructs)
- **HTMT < .90** — liberal criterion (Henseler et al., 2015; theoretically related constructs)
- **HTMT ≥ 1.0** — discriminant validity definitively fails

Given that your factor correlation is **r ≈ .68** (√.46), your HTMT will likely land somewhere in the **.72–.82 range** depending on the heterotrait correlations — potentially close to or above the .85 threshold. You need to compute it explicitly.

### Additional Evidence Worth Reporting

1. **Bootstrap CI on HTMT** — If the 90% CI excludes 1.0, that's formal support for discriminant validity (Henseler et al., 2015). `semTools::htmt()` can return confidence intervals via bootstrapping.

2. **Factor correlation CI** — Does the 95% CI around *r* = .68 exclude 1.0? It almost certainly does, but reporting this is a low-cost supplement.

3. **Model comparison (Δχ²)** — Compare your freely estimated model against a model with the factor correlation constrained to 1.0. A significant Δχ² supports discriminant validity. *Caveat:* this test is sensitive to sample size and doesn't perform well with large N.
