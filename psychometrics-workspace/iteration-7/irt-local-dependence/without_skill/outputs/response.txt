## The Core Problem: Global Fit ≠ Dimensionality Test

The M2, RMSEA.IRT, and CFI are **global fit** statistics. They assess whether your unidimensional model adequately reproduces the observed **bivariate item relationships** (marginal proportions and pairwise associations). Passing this test tells you the model is *not grossly wrong*. It does not tell you it is *dimensionally correct*.

A correlated two-factor model or bifactor model would often pass the exact same global fit thresholds — sometimes with *better* fit. Global fit statistics cannot distinguish between a genuinely unidimensional structure and a multidimensional structure that your model is approximating well enough to avoid gross misfit.

---

## Specific Technical Gaps in the Current Evidence

### 1. No direct dimensionality analysis
Unidimensionality should be evaluated *before or alongside* IRT model fitting using:
- **Parallel analysis** on the tetrachoric correlation matrix (not Pearson — your items are binary/ordinal)
- **EFA** on tetrachoric correlations: examine eigenvalue ratios. A strong general factor shows a dominant first eigenvalue dwarfing the second; a ratio of roughly ≥ 4:1 is a common (though not universal) heuristic
- **DIMTEST** or **DETECT** — designed specifically to detect IRT-based multidimensionality
- A **bifactor model** comparison (unidimensional vs. bifactor via AIC/BIC or likelihood ratio test)

### 2. No local dependence (LD) check
The local independence assumption is foundational to IRT person parameter estimation. Global fit can look fine while specific item pairs exhibit substantial LD (e.g., item pairs sharing a passage, a theme, or a sub-skill). In `mirt` you can extract:

```r
residuals(mod, type = "Q3")        # Yen's Q3 statistic for all item pairs
residuals(mod, type = "LD")        # Chen & Thissen LD χ² statistics
```

Q3 values > .20 (above the baseline-adjusted mean) signal meaningful LD. Even a few pairs with high LD can inflate reliability estimates and bias θ̂ (person parameter) precision — exactly what you're about to estimate.

### 3. "Confirms" is statistically incoherent
Null hypothesis logic applies here. Your M2 test is:

- **H₀**: The unidimensional 2PL model fits the data
- **Result**: Fail to reject H₀ (p = .153)

Failing to reject H₀ is **not** confirmation of H₀. It means the data are *consistent with* the model, given your sample size and the test's power. A small or moderate sample will routinely fail to detect real multidimensionality with these statistics.

## What You Should Do Before Proceeding

| Check | Tool in `mirt` / R | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Parallel analysis | `psych::fa.parallel()` on `psych::tetrachoric()` | How many factors above the simulated scree? |
| EFA on tetrachoric r | `psych::fa(..., cor="tet")` | Eigenvalue ratio λ₁/λ₂; factor loadings on residual factors |
| Bifactor model fit | `mirt(..., model=bifactor_spec)` | AIC/BIC vs. unidimensional; ωh |
| Local dependence | `residuals(mod, type="Q3")` | Pairs with Q3 > .20 (adjusted) |
| McDonald's ωh | `psych::omega()` on tetrachoric r | ωh ≥ .70–.80 supports a dominant general factor |
| Model comparison | `anova(mod1d, mod_bifactor)` | LRT p-value + ΔAIC/ΔBIC |

## The Bottom Line

Your global fit results are **encouraging** — they suggest no catastrophic model misfit — but they are **insufficient** to confirm unidimensionality or to justify skipping local dependence diagnostics. The appropriate statement is:

> *"The unidimensional 2PL model shows acceptable global fit. We additionally conducted parallel analysis, examined Q3 residuals for local dependence, and compared the unidimensional model to a bifactor alternative to evaluate the plausibility of the unidimensionality assumption."*

Proceeding directly to person parameter estimation without these checks risks:
- **Biased θ̂ estimates** if a meaningful secondary dimension exists
- **Overestimated precision** (underestimated SEs) if local dependence is present
- **Construct validity problems** if the "general factor" is not as general as assumed
