Your CFA setup looks mostly reasonable, but there is one thing worth flagging: you are using ML (maximum likelihood) estimation with 5-point Likert items. ML assumes your data are continuous and normally distributed, which ordinal Likert items technically are not. For ordinal indicators, WLSMV (weighted least squares, mean- and variance-adjusted) is generally recommended because it works with polychoric correlations and does not assume normality.

In lavaan, you can switch to WLSMV like this:

```r
fit <- cfa(model, data = d, ordered = c("item1", "item2", ..., "item12"))
```

Declaring items as ordered automatically triggers WLSMV.

As for your fit indices — CFI = .94 and RMSEA = .058 — those are in the acceptable range by conventional benchmarks (CFI > .90 is acceptable, > .95 is good; RMSEA < .08 is acceptable, < .06 is good). The model appears to fit reasonably. However, since these were obtained with ML, the values could shift somewhat if you rerun with WLSMV.

Overall: your model structure may be fine, but the estimator choice is worth revisiting. WLSMV is the more defensible option for Likert data.

In practice, the difference between ML and WLSMV for 5-point Likert scales is often not dramatic — particularly when the item distributions are reasonably symmetric and the sample is large. Simulation studies have shown that ML can perform adequately with five response categories under these conditions.

Your advisor may have a point that the practical impact in your specific case is minimal. If your items are not heavily skewed and your model fit is good, the conclusions you draw are unlikely to change meaningfully if you switched to WLSMV.

That said, if reviewers or other readers with psychometric expertise look at the manuscript, using ML on ordinal items is the kind of thing that can attract criticism. A few options to consider:

1. Keep ML but acknowledge the limitation clearly in your methods section, noting that WLSMV is an alternative for ordinal data and that the substantive conclusions are not expected to change materially.
2. Run WLSMV as a supplementary robustness check and report it in a table or footnote.
3. Simply keep ML as requested by reviewers, noting the estimator and its assumptions in the methods.

Given the reviewer request and your advisor's assessment, keeping ML is not unreasonable here. The estimator choice is a technical detail that may not affect your substantive conclusions.
