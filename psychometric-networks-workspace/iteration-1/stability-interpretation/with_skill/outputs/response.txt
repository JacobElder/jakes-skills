## CS-coefficient decisions

The field thresholds (Epskamp, Borsboom & Fried, 2018) give three zones: ≥0.50 = acceptable, ≥0.25 = minimum to interpret, <0.25 = do not interpret.

| Index | CS | Zone | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expected Influence | 0.44 | Between min and acceptable | Interpret with explicit hedging |
| Strength | 0.36 | Between min and acceptable | Interpret with explicit hedging |
| Closeness | 0.13 | Below minimum | **Do not report or interpret** |
| Betweenness | 0.05 | Far below minimum | **Do not report or interpret** |

Drop Closeness and Betweenness entirely — this is actually the correct outcome, not a failure. The skill's guidance is that these indices are almost never stable in dense weighted partial-correlation graphs and weren't designed for this use case (Bringmann et al., 2019). Reviewers who know the literature will expect them to be absent.

For Strength (0.36) and Expected Influence (0.44), you're in the "interpretable but fragile" zone. You can rank nodes by these indices but you should:

- **Use ordinal language only**: "Node X showed relatively higher strength than nodes Y and Z" — not "Node X was the most central symptom."
- **Run the bootstrapped difference tests** and only highlight contrasts where the CIs on the difference don't cross zero. The raw ranking on the centrality plot is not sufficient justification for calling out specific nodes.
- **Report the CS-coefficients explicitly** in your results section, not just in supplementary material.

## What the wide, overlapping edge-weight CIs mean

Wide and overlapping CIs on edge weights are extremely common with n < ~500 for an 18-node network. What they tell you:

1. **You cannot reliably rank most individual edges.** If two edges' bootstrapped CIs overlap substantially, you cannot claim one is stronger than the other — use the difference test output (`differenceTest()` in bootnet) to identify which edge pairs *are* distinguishable.
2. **The presence/absence of edges (the regularized zeros) may be more trustworthy than the weight ordering.** EBICglasso's main job is sparsity — edges set to exactly zero are likely genuinely absent or negligible. The *structure* (which nodes are connected at all) is a more defensible claim than the *ranking of weights*.
3. **Absolute weight values are not interpretable.** Don't say "the edge between nodes A and B was strong (weight = 0.42)." Partial correlations from a regularized estimator are shrunk, and without knowing where in the CI distribution 0.42 sits, it carries no standalone meaning.

## What you can responsibly report

**Claim confidently:**
- The overall network structure (which connections survive regularization), flagged as exploratory.
- Global network descriptives: density, number of edges, mean edge weight.
- The identity of the top 2–3 nodes by Expected Influence *if* the bootstrapped difference tests show they are distinguishable from the rest — and only with hedged language.
- The CS-coefficients themselves as a transparency measure.

**Claim with explicit hedging:**
- Centrality ordering for Strength and EI, framed as "preliminary" or "tentative," acknowledging CS < 0.50.
- Any edge-weight comparisons that survive the difference test.

**Do not claim:**
- Causal importance of high-centrality nodes — CS of 0.36–0.44 doesn't support that even descriptively, and cross-sectional GGMs don't support it at all.
- Any interpretation of Closeness or Betweenness.
- That specific edge weights are meaningfully different from one another without a passing difference test.

## Suggested write-up language

> *Centrality analyses revealed relatively higher expected influence for [nodes X, Y] and relatively higher strength for [nodes X, Y]; however, CS-coefficients for both indices fell below the acceptable threshold of 0.50 (EI: CS = 0.44; Strength: CS = 0.36), indicating moderate instability. These findings should be interpreted with caution and treated as hypothesis-generating. Closeness (CS = 0.13) and betweenness (CS = 0.05) fell below the minimum interpretable threshold of 0.25 and are not reported. Bootstrapped edge-weight confidence intervals were wide with substantial overlap across most pairwise comparisons, precluding confident ranking of individual edges; the estimated network structure (pattern of zero vs. nonzero edges) is accordingly treated as the primary result.*

## One diagnostic check worth running

If you haven't yet, run `plot(bootnet(net, type = "case"), statistics = c("strength", "expectedInfluence"))` to see *which nodes*, when dropped, are destabilizing the centrality. Sometimes instability traces back to a small cluster of influential cases or to one or two nodes with unusual response distributions — that's actionable information for revisions.

The bottom line: your network's structure is reportable; its centrality is reportable with hedging; specific edge magnitudes are mostly not. That's a common and honest outcome for this sample-size range, and framing it that way is exactly what the Burger et al. (2023) reporting standards in *Psychological Methods* call for.
