## Model: Multilevel VAR (`mlVAR`)

Your design — multiple people, repeated intensive measurements — maps exactly onto **multilevel vector autoregression**. The canonical package is `mlVAR` (Epskamp, Deserno & Bringmann). `graphicalVAR` is for *single-subject* idiographic designs; with N=80 you want the multilevel version that pools information across persons while estimating person-level random effects.

`mlVAR` produces **three networks simultaneously**:

| Network | Edges | What it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **Temporal** | Directed, lag-1 partial regressions | "Does high anxiety at *t* predict high sadness at *t+1*, controlling for all other emotions?" |
| **Contemporaneous** | Undirected partial correlations (within-occasion residuals) | "Do anxiety and sadness co-occur *within the same beep*, after removing temporal dynamics?" |
| **Between-subjects** | Undirected partial correlations of person-level means | "Do people who are high in average anxiety also tend to be high in average sadness?" |

The temporal and contemporaneous networks are the interpretive focus for your "how emotions influence each other over time" question.

---

## Pre-estimation decisions you must make explicitly

### 1. Block overnight lags
This is the single most common error in ESM network papers. Your lag-1 predictor at beep 1 of day 2 would otherwise be beep 5 of day 1 — but those are separated by ~12 hours, not ~3. Standard practice is to set a `day` variable and exclude cross-day lags:

```r
library(mlVAR)

# Mark the first beep of each day so mlVAR drops overnight lags
data$beepDay <- paste(data$participantID, data$day, sep = "_")
# mlVAR's 'dayvar' argument handles this automatically
```

### 2. Within-person centering
`mlVAR` grand-mean-centers by default. For affect items you almost certainly want **within-person centering** (each person's own mean subtracted), which separates within-person dynamics from between-person mean differences. Pass `centerWithin = "person"` (the default in recent versions — confirm for your mlVAR version).

### 3. Check for trends / non-stationarity
ESM mood data sometimes drifts over 14 days (participant adaptation, study fatigue). Visually inspect mean affect over days per person. If you see systematic trends, detrend before modeling or include a session/day covariate.

## Minimal pipeline

```r
library(mlVAR)

fit <- mlVAR(
  data,
  vars      = c("relaxed", "anxious", "sad", "happy",   # your 8 items
                "irritable", "energetic", "content", "worried"),
  idvar     = "participantID",
  lags      = 1,
  dayvar    = "day",          # blocks overnight lags — do not skip
  beepvar   = "beep",         # beep number within day
  estimator = "lmer",         # default; "corr" is faster but less accurate
  contemporaneous = "correlated",
  between   = "correlated"
)

# Plot the three networks
plot(fit, "temporal",       layout = "circle", title = "Temporal")
plot(fit, "contemporaneous", layout = "circle", title = "Contemporaneous")
plot(fit, "between",         layout = "circle", title = "Between-subjects")

# Summary of fixed effects
summary(fit)
```

The `estimator = "lmer"` option estimates random effects properly; `"corr"` is much faster and useful for exploration but shouldn't be your final reported model.

## Centrality — what to report and what to skip

**Temporal network** (directed edges):

- Report **out-strength** (how strongly a node drives others) and **in-strength** (how strongly a node is driven by others) — these are directionally meaningful.
- The **diagonal** (auto-regression coefficients / self-loops) reflects **emotional inertia** — how much each emotion predicts itself. High inertia is associated with psychopathology in the Bringmann et al. tradition. Report these separately from cross-lagged edges.
- Avoid betweenness and closeness (same reasons as cross-sectional GGMs — not appropriate for dense weighted directed graphs).

**Contemporaneous network** (undirected):

- Report **strength** or **expected influence** (use Expected Influence if any edges are negative — likely for mixed-valence affect items).

**Between-subjects network:**

- Centrality is usually not the focus here; the network itself is the result.

## Accuracy and stability — what exists for mlVAR

The `bootnet` pipeline doesn't apply directly to `mlVAR` the way it does for cross-sectional GGMs. The current field practice is:

1. **Report fixed-effect confidence intervals** from `summary(fit)` — these come from the `lmer` estimation directly.
2. **Report the random effects variance** for each edge — this tells you how much individual networks vary around the group network. High variance means the group network is a poor summary of any individual. With N=80 you have some power to detect this.
3. **Report person-level network heterogeneity** — you can extract individual networks with `getNet(fit, "temporal", subject = TRUE)` and plot the distribution of edge weights. If individuals vary wildly, note this as a limitation of group-level centrality interpretation.
4. **Do not over-interpret specific edge weights** — with 80 participants and 8 nodes, the random effects are estimated from 80 data points each. Individual differences in specific edges will be uncertain.

There is no CS-coefficient analog for mlVAR as of the published literature — be explicit in your write-up that the cross-sectional stability framework doesn't directly transfer.

## Reporting checklist for your write-up

- [ ] N participants, beeps/day, total days, total planned observations, **observed completion rate / % missing**
- [ ] Node selection rationale (why these 8 items, what constructs)
- [ ] `mlVAR` version, `estimator = "lmer"`, centering method
- [ ] Overnight-lag exclusion (dayvar argument) — state this explicitly
- [ ] Stationarity check / whether detrending was applied
- [ ] All three network plots with layout specified
- [ ] Fixed-effect edge-weight table (or supplement) with confidence intervals
- [ ] Random effects variance per node reported (assess between-person heterogeneity)
- [ ] Centrality: out-strength and in-strength for temporal; strength/EI for contemporaneous
- [ ] Auto-regression diagonal reported separately as emotional inertia
- [ ] Explicit acknowledgment that temporal edges are not causal without randomized intervention

## Key references

- **Bringmann et al. (2013)** *Psychotherapy and Psychosomatics* — original mlVAR for ESM paper; the foundational reference.
- **Haslbeck, Bringmann & Waldorp (2021)** *Multivariate Behavioral Research* — "A Tutorial on Estimating Time-Varying Vector Autoregressive Models"; covers extensions.
- **Epskamp, Deserno & Bringmann (2022)** — the `mlVAR` package paper (check CRAN documentation for the current citation).
- **Bringmann et al. (2016)** *Psychological Medicine* — emotional inertia and the auto-regression diagonal.
- **Isvoranu et al. (2022)** *Network Psychometrics with R* — Chapter on temporal networks is the cleanest pedagogical treatment.

One practical note: with 80 participants × 70 beeps × 8 nodes, your data are reasonable for the fixed effects but the random effects (person-level network heterogeneity) will be estimated with meaningful uncertainty. Consider whether your research question is truly about the **group-level** network or about **individual differences** in network structure — the latter requires substantially larger N or a different analytic frame (e.g., idiographic `graphicalVAR` per person, then meta-analysis of person networks).
