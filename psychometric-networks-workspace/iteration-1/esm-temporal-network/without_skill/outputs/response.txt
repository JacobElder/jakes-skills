## Model Recommendation: Multilevel Vector Autoregressive (mlVAR)

Your design is a textbook case for **multilevel VAR**, specifically the `mlVAR` package in R (Epskamp et al., 2018). Here's the full picture:

---

### Why mlVAR

You have two problems simultaneously:
1. **Temporal dynamics** — emotions at time *t* predicting emotions at *t+1* (requires VAR)
2. **Nested data** — 70 time points per person, 80 people (requires multilevel structure)

mlVAR handles both. It estimates a lag-1 VAR model for each person using a multilevel framework, so you get **group-level (fixed) effects** plus **random effects** capturing individual variation in dynamics.

A pure N=1 VAR per person is tempting but inadvisable here — 70 observations estimating 8×8 = 64 lagged parameters leaves almost no degrees of freedom. The multilevel partial pooling is doing real work.

---

### The Three Networks You'll Get

mlVAR produces three networks, each answering a different question:

| Network | What it captures | Edge type |
|---|---|---|
| **Temporal** | Does emotion X at *t* predict emotion Y at *t+1*, controlling for all others? | Directed, weighted |
| **Contemporaneous** | Are emotions X and Y correlated *within the same beep*, after removing lagged effects? | Undirected, partial correlations |
| **Between-persons** | Do people high on trait X tend to be high on trait Y? | Undirected, from random effect means |

The temporal network is your primary object of inference for "how emotions influence each other over time."

---

### Critical Pre-Processing Decisions

These need to be explicit in your methods:

**1. Handle day boundaries**
The lag between Day 1 beep 5 and Day 2 beep 1 involves an overnight gap — it's not the same interval as within-day lags. Standard practice: include a variable that flags these cross-day transitions and pass it to mlVAR as the `dayvar` argument so those lags are excluded from estimation.

**2. Person-mean center your items**
Before fitting, subtract each person's mean from their own time series. This removes stable between-person differences (person A is generally happier than person B) and focuses the VAR on *within-person* fluctuations, which is what you want. mlVAR does this internally by default — make sure it's on.

**3. Confirm lag-1 is appropriate**
With 5x/day spacing (~2–3 hour intervals), lag-1 is standard and theoretically defensible. You could fit lag-2 as a sensitivity check, but models get sparse fast.

---

### What to Report

**Model specification section:**
- Software: `mlVAR` version X in R; REML estimation
- Lag order (1), how day boundaries were handled, whether person-mean centering was applied
- Number of usable observations after dropping cross-day lags (will be ~80 persons × 4 within-day lags × 14 days = ~4,480 lagged observations)

**Temporal network:**
- Fixed-effect lagged coefficients (the 8×8 matrix) — report as a network figure with directed edges, weighted by coefficient magnitude, thresholded at some principled cutoff (e.g., |β| > 0.05 or FDR-corrected)
- **Autoregressive effects** (diagonal): how much does each affect item predict itself? High AR coefficients = emotional inertia
- **Cross-lagged effects** (off-diagonal): the between-emotion predictive relationships
- Node-level centrality: **out-strength** (how much does this emotion drive others?) and **in-strength** (how much is this emotion driven by others?) — avoid betweenness/closeness, which are unreliable in small signed networks
- Variance of random effects for each fixed effect — quantifies how much individual differences in dynamics exist

**Contemporaneous network:**
- Estimated from partial correlations of within-time residuals
- Same visualization approach; report node centrality (strength)

**Between-persons network:**
- Estimated from the random intercept means across participants
- Useful for situating your temporal findings: does the trait-level structure match the dynamic structure?

**Model stability:**
- Report the **stability of the VAR** by checking that all eigenvalues of the fixed-effect coefficient matrix lie inside the unit circle (implies the process is stationary). The `qgraph` package can compute this. If eigenvalues are near 1.0, flag it — the system may be approaching instability, which is theoretically interesting in affect research.

**Individual differences:**
- Variance components (random effect SDs) for key paths
- Optionally: correlate individual AR coefficient estimates (extracted via `$results`) with person-level traits if you have them

---

### Alternative Worth Knowing: DSEM in Mplus

If you have substantial missing data (>15–20% of beeps), or want to model covariates of the lagged effects (e.g., "does neuroticism moderate emotional inertia?"), **Dynamic SEM** in Mplus handles this more gracefully. It's more work to set up but gives you full latent-variable flexibility. For a clean ESM dataset focused on network structure, mlVAR is the right call.
