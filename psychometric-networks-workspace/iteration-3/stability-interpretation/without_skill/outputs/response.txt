## CS-Coefficients: What Each One Tells You

The CS-coefficient (Epskamp et al., 2018) is the maximum proportion of cases droppable while still maintaining a ≥0.70 correlation between the original and resampled centrality *order*. Thresholds: **≥0.50 = good**, **≥0.25 = acceptable minimum**, **<0.25 = do not interpret**.

| Index | CS | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Expected Influence | 0.44 | ✅ Acceptable — report with minor caveat |
| Strength | 0.36 | ⚠️ Marginal — report cautiously, flag instability |
| Closeness | 0.13 | ❌ Below threshold — do **not** interpret |
| Betweenness | 0.05 | ❌ Well below threshold — do **not** interpret |

The Closeness and Betweenness results are common with smaller/denser networks — they're notoriously unstable in psychological network studies and you're in good company dropping them entirely.

---

## Edge Weight CIs: What Wide + Overlapping Means

Wide, overlapping bootstrapped CIs are the norm, not an anomaly. They tell you two distinct things:

1. **Accuracy** (non-parametric bootstrap): Wide CIs mean individual edge weight *estimates* are imprecise. You cannot reliably rank-order most edges by magnitude.
2. **Difference test** (the key diagnostic): Even if individual CIs are wide, the **bootstrapped difference between two specific edges** can still be significant. Run `differenceTest()` in bootnet/qgraph — only edges that survive the difference test should be compared.

---

## What You Can Responsibly Report

**✅ Report freely:**
- Network topology (which edges are present/absent after GLASSO regularization) — edge *presence* is more stable than edge *weight*
- Expected Influence rankings, with CS=0.44 noted
- Strength rankings, with the caveat that CS=0.36 is below the ideal threshold and ordinal comparisons should be made cautiously

**⚠️ Report with explicit caveats:**
- Specific edge weights — state that CIs are wide and most pairwise weight comparisons are non-significant; avoid language like "X was the strongest edge"
- Strength as a centrality index — note marginal stability

**❌ Do not report/interpret:**
- Closeness or Betweenness centrality rankings at all — flag them as unstable and exclude from substantive interpretation
- Any claim that specific nodes are "hubs" based on betweenness

## One More Thing to Consider

With 18 nodes, your GLASSO network has a *lot* of potential edges (153). If your *n* is below ~300–500, edge weight instability is largely a sample size problem, not a modeling failure. Reporting the *structure* of the network (presence/absence) is more defensible than any quantitative weight comparison. You might also consider reporting the network under multiple EBIC γ tuning values (e.g., 0, 0.25, 0.50) to show how sensitive the structure is to regularization strength.
