## Node Selection in Psychopathology Networks: The Conceptual Issues

This is one of the sharpest methodological critiques in the network literature, and the reviewer is right to push on it. Here's how to think through it carefully.

---

### The Core Problem: Networks Don't Come with "Natural" Nodes

Unlike, say, a social network where nodes are clearly defined individuals, psychopathology networks require you to *impose* a granularity. The same OCD phenomenology could be represented as:

- 10 Y-BOCS items
- 5 obsession items + 5 compulsion items (subscale level)
- Y-BOCS Symptom Checklist items (~58 symptom-specific categories)
- Two nodes: obsessions, compulsions
- One node: OCD severity

Each level of granularity produces a different network with different edges, different centrality estimates, and potentially different clinical implications. The reviewer wants to know why *your* level is theoretically defensible, not just convenient.

---

### The Specific Problem with Y-BOCS Items as Nodes

This is where Y-BOCS creates a deeper issue than most scales. The 10 items don't represent distinct *symptom types* — they represent **severity dimensions applied twice** (once to obsessions, once to compulsions):

| Dimension | Obsession item | Compulsion item |
|---|---|---|
| Time occupied | O1 | C1 |
| Interference | O2 | C2 |
| Distress | O3 | C3 |
| Resistance | O4 | C4 |
| Control | O5 | C5 |

This creates a conceptual tension: network theory (in the Borsboom mutualist tradition) assumes nodes are **causally relevant, relatively distinct symptom components** that can activate each other. But Y-BOCS items are really severity facets of the same two symptom clusters. The question a skeptic would ask: *are these 10 nodes, or are they 10 measurements of 2 things?*

This matters because if the Y-BOCS items are better understood as reflective indicators of underlying obsession severity and compulsion severity, your network may be recovering latent factor structure rather than genuine symptom interactions. That's the latent variable vs. network debate (Borsboom & Cramer, 2013; Schmittmann et al., 2013) applied directly to your design.

---

### How to Justify the Choice

You have several defensible arguments — use them in combination:

**1. Theoretical: distinguish severity dimensions from symptom components**
Argue that each Y-BOCS dimension captures a *mechanistically distinct* facet. Time spent and distress, for instance, may be causally separable: one can experience high distress with brief intrusions, and the resistance/control items tap into a volitional component that's theoretically upstream of the others. Cite work on the distinction between ego-dystonic distress and behavioral compulsion as potentially independent processes.

**2. Structural: the obsession/compulsion architecture is meaningful**
The parallel structure (O1–O5, C1–C5) is a feature, not a bug, if your research question involves *how obsession severity feeds into compulsion severity and vice versa*. The within-obsession and within-compulsion edges versus cross-construct edges is a legitimate network-theoretic question (McNally et al., 2017 did exactly this).

**3. Psychometric precedent**
The Y-BOCS has known factor structure (typically 2-factor), which you can use to frame your network as an alternative representation of the same data. Some papers explicitly compare network and factor models on the same items as a robustness check — that framing neutralizes the critique.

**4. Practical comparability**
The Y-BOCS is the gold standard outcome measure; most clinical trials and large datasets use it. If your goal is to produce findings with clinical translation or meta-analytic comparability, this is a genuine justification, not just a convenience argument.

---

### What to Acknowledge as a Limitation

Don't try to fully refute the reviewer — acknowledge cleanly:

- The Y-BOCS does not capture **symptom content heterogeneity** (contamination, harm obsessions, symmetry, etc.); networks built on the Symptom Checklist would tell a different story about OCD's architecture
- The parallel severity-dimension structure means some edges may reflect **measurement overlap** rather than causal relationships between distinct constructs
- Results should be interpreted as characterizing **OCD severity structure**, not necessarily the full symptom-level causal network

---

### The Bigger Frame to Give the Reviewer

The most honest framing is something like:

> *Node selection is a theoretical commitment, not merely a measurement decision. We treat Y-BOCS items as operationalizations of distinct severity-relevant processes (time, interference, distress, resistance, control) within the obsession and compulsion domains. We acknowledge this granularity does not capture symptom content heterogeneity, which would require a checklist-based approach. Our network characterizes the dimensional structure of OCD severity and the interdependencies between these facets — a complementary, not exhaustive, representation of OCD's architecture.*

That framing is honest, theoretically grounded, and pre-empts the follow-up critique about what you're *not* capturing.

---

**Key papers worth citing in your justification:**
- Borsboom & Cramer (2013) — *Annual Review of Clinical Psychology* (the theoretical foundation)
- McNally et al. (2017) — *Psychological Medicine* (OCD network with Y-BOCS items)
- Fried & Nesse (2015) — on why item-level analysis matters
- Haslbeck, Ryan, & Dablander (2021) — on the problem of model selection and node granularity
