## Strength vs. Expected Influence for your affect network

The short answer for your specific data: **report Expected Influence as your primary index**, and either drop Strength or relegate it to supplementary material. Here's the reasoning.

---

### The core difference

| Index | Formula | What it ignores |
|---|---|---|
| **Strength** | Σ \|w_ij\| | Sign of each edge |
| **Expected Influence** | Σ w_ij | Nothing — signed sum |

Strength treats a strong *negative* edge and a strong *positive* edge identically: both inflate a node's centrality. Expected Influence preserves direction, so negative edges *reduce* a node's EI rather than inflate it.

---

### Why this matters for your specific network

Your 8-item design almost guarantees negative edges. In a GGM of PANAS-type items, EBICglasso will typically produce:

- **Positive edges within the PA cluster** (enthusiastic–inspired, active–alert, etc.)
- **Positive edges within the NA cluster** (distressed–upset, hostile–afraid, etc.)
- **Negative edges across clusters** (e.g., inspired–distressed, alert–afraid)

This is the canonical case where Strength misleads. Consider what happens to a node like **distressed**:

- It has strong positive edges to upset, hostile, and afraid → these inflate Strength *and* EI
- It has moderate negative edges to enthusiastic, active, inspired, alert → these inflate Strength but *reduce* EI

Strength would make distressed look more central than it is in terms of net network influence. EI correctly captures that distressed has a partially dampening relationship with the other half of the network.

---

### Practical interpretation guide

**Expected Influence** answers: *If this node were activated (or elevated), what is the net directional push on the rest of the network?*

- High positive EI → node activation tends to activate neighbors (net positive)
- EI near zero → activation effects roughly cancel across positive and negative connections
- Negative EI → counterintuitive but interpretable: this node's connections run mostly opposite-sign to the dominant direction (rare, but possible for bridge nodes)

**Strength** answers: *How densely connected is this node, ignoring direction?*

This is a reasonable question if you only care about overall involvement in the network, but in a mixed-valence affect network it conflates two very different things — co-activation within a valence cluster and cross-cluster suppression — into one number.

---

### Practical steps before finalizing

1. **Inspect your edge matrix.** Confirm negative edges actually appear (they almost certainly do). If by chance your regularization is aggressive enough that all cross-cluster edges dropped to zero, the Strength/EI distinction collapses and you have a different problem (likely over-regularization).

2. **Check your CS-coefficients for both indices.** With only 8 nodes you may actually have adequate stability for EI, but confirm — the case-dropping bootstrap is the check, not node count alone.

3. **Consider bridge centrality.** With a clean PA/NA community structure, `networktools::bridge()` may tell you more than EI alone — it quantifies which specific nodes sit at the interface between the two clusters. This is a natural complement to EI for your design and directly connects to the question of which affect states might serve as bridges between positive and negative mood spirals.

---

### Reporting language

> "We report Expected Influence (Robinaugh et al., 2016) as the primary centrality index, as the network contains theoretically meaningful negative edges between positive- and negative-affect items; Strength, which sums absolute edge weights, would conflate within-valence co-activation with cross-valence suppression."

That one sentence is all you need to justify the choice to reviewers.
