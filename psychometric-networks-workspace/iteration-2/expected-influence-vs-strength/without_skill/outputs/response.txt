## Strength vs. Expected Influence in Affect Networks

This is a meaningful methodological choice, not just a cosmetic one — and your bipolar structure (PA vs. NA) makes it particularly consequential.

---

### What each measure actually computes

| Measure | Formula | What it captures |
|---|---|---|
| **Strength** | Σ \|w_ij\| (sum of **absolute** edge weights) | Overall connectivity; how "busy" a node is |
| **Expected Influence (EI)** | Σ w_ij (sum of **signed** edge weights) | Net directional pull; whether activation of a node tends to spread or suppress |

The critical word is *absolute* vs. *signed*. Strength treats a strong negative edge identically to a strong positive edge. EI lets them cancel.

---

### Why this matters for your specific network

Your 8-item network has a predictable edge sign structure:

- **Within PA** (enthusiastic ↔ active ↔ inspired ↔ alert): predominantly **positive** edges
- **Within NA** (distressed ↔ upset ↔ hostile ↔ afraid): predominantly **positive** edges
- **Between PA and NA**: predominantly **negative** edges (the classic bipolar structure)

This creates a situation where **Strength and EI can diverge substantially for bridge nodes** — items that have strong positive within-cluster ties *and* strong negative cross-cluster ties. Those nodes accumulate high Strength (lots of absolute weight) but middling or even negative EI (the two directions cancel out).

Concrete example: suppose "active" has strong positive ties to the other PA items (+0.4, +0.35, +0.3) but also moderate negative ties to the NA items (−0.2, −0.15, −0.1, −0.1). Its **Strength is high** (sum of absolutes ≈ 1.6), but its **EI is lower** (net signed sum ≈ +0.5) because the negative cross-community edges eat into it.

---

### Which to report, and why

**Report both — but lead with EI for theoretical interpretation, and use Strength for robustness checks.**

Here's the reasoning:

**Use EI as your primary centrality** when:
- You're making claims about *intervention targets* or *dynamics* (which item, if elevated, most strongly activates the rest of the network?)
- You care about *valence-sensitive influence* — EI preserves the theoretically important distinction between activating-positive and activating-negative effects
- Haslbeck & Fried (2017) showed EI (one-step) better predicts node intervention effects in simulations than Strength does

**Strength remains useful for**:
- Reporting *overall hub-ness* in a way that's agnostic to sign (useful if you're uncertain about edge sign reliability)
- Showing readers the raw connectivity pattern before the signed story
- Checking whether Strength and EI rankings agree — large disagreements flag nodes that are heavily bridging the PA/NA communities

**If they agree**, your conclusions are robust to the choice — report both briefly and note the convergence.  
**If they disagree**, that disagreement is itself a finding worth interpreting (those discordant nodes are bridge items with high structural importance but neutralized net influence, or vice versa).

---

### Practical reporting template

> "We computed two centrality indices: Strength (sum of absolute edge weights) and one-step Expected Influence (sum of signed edge weights; Robinaugh et al., 2016). In networks containing negative edges — as expected in bipolar affect structures — these measures can diverge; nodes with high cross-valence connectivity accumulate high Strength but attenuated Expected Influence. We use Expected Influence as the primary index for theoretical interpretation and Strength as a descriptive complement."

---

### One additional consideration

Are you using the **qgraph/bootnet** defaults? By default, `qgraph` plots **one-step EI**. There's also **two-step EI** (which propagates through neighbors' neighbors), but for an 8-node network with a clear community structure, one-step is typically preferred — two-step adds complexity without much gain when the graph is small and densely connected.

If your network is estimated with a **GGM (partial correlations)**, also note that edges already condition on all other nodes, so Strength in a GGM reflects unique pairwise connectivity — slightly different interpretation than Strength in a correlation network.
