FINANCIAL ADVICE: Allocating $1,200/Month Given Your Situation
==============================================================

Your situation has a few key features that heavily shape the optimal strategy:
- $18,000 in credit card debt at 24% APR (very high-interest debt)
- $3,000 in savings (thin emergency fund)
- Enrolled in an HDHP (HSA-eligible)
- No employer 401k match (no free money on the table)

Here is what I recommend, in priority order:

---

STEP 1: FUND YOUR HSA FIRST (~$100–$150/month)
-----------------------------------------------
Because you're enrolled in a high-deductible health plan, you're eligible to
contribute to a Health Savings Account (HSA). This is one of the most
tax-advantaged accounts available — contributions are pre-tax, growth is
tax-free, and withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free (triple
tax advantage).

2026 HSA contribution limits:
  - Individual coverage: $4,300/year (~$358/month)
  - Family coverage:     $8,550/year (~$712/month)

However, given your credit card debt situation, I'd suggest contributing a
modest amount — enough to cover expected out-of-pocket medical costs, not the
full maximum right now. A reasonable starting point is $100–$150/month. You
can increase this later once the debt is cleared.

Why do this even with debt? Because medical expenses WILL happen, and paying
them with pre-tax HSA dollars instead of post-tax dollars (or worse, putting
them on the credit card) saves you real money. Keep HSA funds invested
conservatively or in cash for near-term medical use.

---

STEP 2: SHORE UP YOUR EMERGENCY FUND (SHORT-TERM PRIORITY)
-----------------------------------------------------------
With only $3,000 in savings and $18,000 in high-interest debt, you're walking
a financial tightrope. A single car repair or medical bill could force you to
add MORE high-interest debt.

Target: 1 month of essential expenses as a minimum floor (~$2,000–$3,000 for
most people). You may already be close. If your $3,000 covers roughly 1 month
of essentials, that's acceptable to keep as-is while you attack debt.

If your savings are below 1 month of expenses, temporarily direct $200–$300/month
here until you hit that floor, then pivot everything to debt payoff.

---

STEP 3: ATTACK THE CREDIT CARD DEBT AGGRESSIVELY (MAIN PRIORITY)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
This is the most important thing you can do right now.

At 24% APR, your $18,000 in credit card debt is costing you roughly $360/month
in interest alone. That's a guaranteed 24% return on every dollar you put
toward paying it down — far better than any investment you could make.

No stock market strategy, index fund, or savings account beats a guaranteed
24% return. The S&P 500 averages ~10% annually over the long run. Paying off
24% debt first is the mathematically correct move.

Suggested allocation (once emergency fund floor is met):
  - HSA:              ~$125/month
  - Emergency fund:   $0 (if already at 1-month floor) or $200 until funded
  - Credit card debt: $975–$1,075/month

At $975/month applied to $18,000 at 24% APR, you would pay off the debt in
approximately 22–24 months (about 2 years), paying roughly $3,500–$4,000
in total interest. That's much better than the ~$8,700+ in interest you'd
pay making only minimum payments over many years.

Debt payoff strategy: Use the avalanche method — pay minimums on all cards,
then throw every extra dollar at the highest-interest card first. If all your
debt is on one card (or all at similar rates), just hammer it.

---

STEP 4: AFTER DEBT IS PAID OFF — REBUILD AND INVEST
----------------------------------------------------
Once the credit card debt is gone, you free up ~$975/month. Here's what to
do with it:

1. EXPAND EMERGENCY FUND: Build up to 3–6 months of essential expenses.
   At $1,200/month saved, this should take only 3–6 months.

2. MAX YOUR HSA: Contribute up to the annual limit ($4,300 for individuals
   in 2026). Invest HSA funds in low-cost index funds (treat it as a stealth
   retirement account — after age 65 you can withdraw for any reason).

3. CONTRIBUTE TO A ROTH IRA: Since there's no employer match, a Roth IRA
   is your next best vehicle. 2026 limit: $7,000/year (~$583/month).
   Invest in low-cost total market index funds (e.g., FSKAX, VTSAX, VTI).
   Roth is especially valuable if you expect to be in a higher tax bracket
   in retirement, or if you're relatively young.

4. IF INCOME IS ABOVE ROTH LIMITS or you want more tax-deferred space:
   Contribute to a Traditional IRA or a 401k (even without a match, the
   tax deferral is valuable).

5. TAXABLE BROKERAGE: Any remaining funds after maxing tax-advantaged
   accounts go into a taxable brokerage account, again in low-cost index funds.

---

SUMMARY: RECOMMENDED MONTHLY ALLOCATION RIGHT NOW
--------------------------------------------------

  HSA (medical safety net + tax savings):     $125
  Emergency fund top-up (if needed):          $100  (temporary)
  Credit card debt paydown:                   $975
  -----------------------------------------------
  TOTAL:                                    $1,200

Once emergency fund is solid (~1 month of expenses):
  HSA:                                        $125
  Credit card debt:                         $1,075
  -----------------------------------------------
  TOTAL:                                    $1,200

The math is simple: eliminate the 24% debt as fast as possible. It is the
highest-return investment available to you. Every other move — 401k, index
funds, savings accounts — comes second until that debt is gone.

---

KEY PRINCIPLES APPLIED
-----------------------
1. High-interest debt payoff > investing (guaranteed 24% return beats market)
2. Minimum emergency buffer before going full debt-attack mode
3. HSA triple tax advantage is worth capturing even during debt payoff
4. No employer match means no urgency to contribute to 401k right now
5. After debt: HSA → Roth IRA → 401k → taxable brokerage (the investment waterfall)
