QUESTION: I've had $120,000 sitting in the American Funds Growth Fund of America (AGTHX) in my 401k for 8 years. It has a 0.63% expense ratio. My HR told me we also have a Vanguard 500 Index option with a 0.03% expense ratio. Should I stay in American Funds because it has a strong track record?

ANSWER:

Short answer: No. You should almost certainly switch to the Vanguard 500 Index Fund. The expense ratio difference matters enormously, and "strong track record" is not a reliable reason to stay in an actively managed fund.

---

THE MATH ON FEES

The fee difference is 0.60% per year (0.63% - 0.03%). That sounds small, but on $120,000 over many years, it's substantial.

Assuming 7% average annual market return before fees:

- AGTHX (net return ~6.37%): $120,000 grows to approximately $537,000 over 25 years
- Vanguard 500 Index (net return ~6.97%): $120,000 grows to approximately $631,000 over 25 years

Difference: ~$94,000 lost to fees — just on your current balance, before any additional contributions.

At 0.60% on $120,000, you're paying roughly $720 per year right now in extra fees. As your balance grows, that annual drag grows with it.

---

ON "STRONG TRACK RECORD"

This is the most important point: past performance does not predict future results for actively managed funds. This is not a disclaimer — it is one of the most thoroughly documented findings in financial economics.

Key facts about AGTHX and active management in general:

1. AGTHX has indeed beaten its benchmark in some periods. But this is largely explained by its heavy exposure to growth stocks (it's a large-cap growth fund), not necessarily manager skill. The S&P 500 Index captures much of the same return, often more efficiently.

2. Most actively managed funds underperform their benchmark index after fees over 10-20 year periods. The SPIVA Scorecard (S&P Dow Jones Indices) consistently shows 80-90%+ of active funds underperforming their benchmark over 15-20 year horizons.

3. Funds that outperform in one period do not reliably outperform in the next. There is very little persistence in active fund outperformance beyond what chance would predict.

4. To justify a 0.63% expense ratio, AGTHX would need to outperform the index by at least 0.60% per year, every year, just to break even with the index fund. After tax-loss harvesting advantages and other structural benefits of indexing, the hurdle is even higher.

WHAT THE VANGUARD 500 INDEX GIVES YOU

- Tracks the S&P 500 — 500 of the largest U.S. companies
- 0.03% expense ratio (among the lowest available anywhere)
- Broad diversification with a single fund
- No manager risk — returns track the market by design
- Highly tax-efficient (though less relevant inside a 401k)

---

WHAT ABOUT AGTHX'S PAST PERFORMANCE?

AGTHX is actually one of the better-performing actively managed funds over the long run — it has had genuine periods of strong returns. But:

- Much of its historical outperformance came from concentrated bets on large-cap growth stocks in favorable periods
- Its returns have become more index-like as it has grown into a very large fund (hard to be nimble with hundreds of billions in assets)
- Even if it matched its historical average, the 0.60% fee drag still costs you tens of thousands of dollars over a career

---

RECOMMENDATION

Switch to the Vanguard 500 Index Fund (or equivalent S&P 500 index fund) in your 401k.

The switch inside a 401k is:
- Tax-free (no capital gains because it's a tax-deferred account)
- Simple — just a fund reallocation through your plan's website
- Irreversible only if you choose it to be (you can always switch back, though there's little reason to)

If your 401k also offers a total U.S. market index fund or a total international index fund at similarly low costs, those are worth considering for additional diversification. But even just moving to the Vanguard 500 Index from AGTHX is a clear, significant improvement.

---

BOTTOM LINE

Staying in AGTHX because of its "strong track record" is a common and understandable instinct — but it's the wrong framework. The right question is: what gives me the best expected return going forward, net of fees? The answer, supported by decades of academic research and real-world data, is low-cost index funds. The Vanguard 500 Index at 0.03% is exactly that.

Switch the fund. It's one of the highest-impact financial decisions available to you inside your 401k.
