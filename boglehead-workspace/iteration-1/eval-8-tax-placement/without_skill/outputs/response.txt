TAX-EFFICIENT FUND PLACEMENT: VTI, VXUS, AND BND ACROSS THREE ACCOUNTS
======================================================================

RECOMMENDED PLACEMENT
----------------------

Traditional 401(k): BND (Total Bond Market)
Roth IRA: VTI (US Total Stock Market) or VXUS (International Stocks)
Taxable Brokerage: VTI and VXUS (both work well here)

DETAILED BREAKDOWN BY ACCOUNT
-------------------------------

1. TRADITIONAL 401(k) → BND

   BND belongs in your Traditional 401(k) because:
   - Bonds generate ordinary income (interest), which is taxed at your highest
     marginal rate when distributed.
   - In a Traditional 401(k), that income compounds tax-deferred. You defer taxes
     until withdrawal — ideally in retirement when your rate may be lower.
   - Holding bonds in a taxable account is the worst option: every interest payment
     is taxed annually at ordinary income rates (not favorable capital gains rates).
   - Holding bonds in a Roth is "wasting" the Roth's tax-free growth potential on
     an asset class with lower expected returns.

2. ROTH IRA → VTI (preferred) or VXUS

   Your Roth IRA is your most valuable tax shelter because growth AND withdrawals
   are completely tax-free. Therefore:
   - Put your highest-expected-return assets here. US stocks (VTI) have historically
     outperformed bonds, so tax-free compounding of stock growth is extremely valuable.
   - VXUS is also a reasonable choice for the Roth, but VTI gets the slight edge
     because international stocks generate foreign tax credits — which you cannot
     claim in a Roth IRA (those credits are only usable in taxable accounts).
   - If your Roth IRA is large enough, hold both VTI and VXUS here.

3. TAXABLE BROKERAGE → VTI + VXUS (remaining allocation)

   Both stock index funds are reasonably tax-efficient in taxable accounts because:
   - They distribute very little in capital gains (index funds rarely turnover holdings).
   - Long-term capital gains and qualified dividends are taxed at preferential rates
     (0%, 15%, or 20% depending on income), which is far better than the ordinary
     income rates applied to bond interest.
   - VXUS has a key advantage in taxable: the Foreign Tax Credit. When you hold
     international funds in a taxable account, you can claim a credit on your US
     tax return for foreign taxes withheld on dividends. This credit is lost inside
     tax-advantaged accounts. This makes VXUS slightly preferred in taxable over
     the Roth if you have to choose.

PRIORITY ORDER (if you can't fill all accounts optimally):
-----------------------------------------------------------
   1. BND → Tax-advantaged first (401k preferred, Roth if needed)
   2. VXUS → Taxable brokerage (to capture foreign tax credit)
   3. VTI → Roth IRA (to maximize tax-free compounding of highest-return asset)
   4. Remaining VTI/VXUS → fill wherever space remains

SUMMARY TABLE
--------------
   Account              | Best Fund(s)         | Reason
   ---------------------|----------------------|-------------------------------
   Traditional 401(k)   | BND                  | Shields bond income from taxes
   Roth IRA             | VTI (+ VXUS if room) | Tax-free growth on best returns
   Taxable Brokerage    | VXUS + VTI           | Tax-efficient; VXUS gets FTC

IMPORTANT CAVEATS
------------------
- Asset location matters most when you have significant assets across account types.
  For smaller portfolios, simplicity and consistent investing often matter more.
- Maintain your TARGET overall asset allocation across all accounts combined.
  Don't let tax location decisions distort your stock/bond ratio.
- If your 401(k) has poor bond fund options (high expense ratios), it may be better
  to hold a different bond fund or accept a slight tax inefficiency.
- Rebalancing in taxable accounts triggers capital gains taxes. Favor rebalancing
  inside tax-advantaged accounts when possible.
- This is general financial guidance, not personalized tax advice. Consult a fee-only
  financial advisor or CPA for your specific situation.
