You're not wrong — and you're actually in good company with some serious investing thinkers. Let me give you an honest take.

The core of your intuition is sound. At 25 with a 40-year horizon, time is your most powerful asset. Bonds exist primarily to reduce volatility and preserve capital, which matters a lot more when you're close to needing the money. When you have four decades of compounding ahead of you, the drag from bonds is very real: historically, a total stock market fund like VTI has returned around 10% annually, while bonds have returned closer to 4-5%. That gap compounds enormously over 40 years.

Jack Bogle himself — the founder of Vanguard and the godfather of index investing — held an unusually high equity allocation for most of his life. The classic "100 minus your age in bonds" rule is increasingly seen as outdated, especially now that people live longer and face low bond yields.

That said, here's where your coworkers and parents aren't entirely off base:

The risk is behavioral, not mathematical. The math clearly favors 100% equities over 40 years. The risk is YOU. When the market drops 40-50% — which it will at some point — will you hold, or will you panic-sell? A portfolio that goes from $100k to $55k is terrifying to live through, even when you intellectually know it will recover. If bonds help you sleep at night and stay the course, they're worth the drag. An investor who holds 80/20 through a crash beats one who holds 100% equities and sells at the bottom.

For a 25-year-old with genuine long-term conviction and no foreseeable need for the money, 100% VTI is defensible. The main questions to honestly ask yourself:

1. Is all your invested money truly money you won't need for 10+ years? If there's any chance you'd need to tap it for a house, emergency, or life change, you want some stability.

2. How did you actually feel during the 2020 COVID crash, or if you've lived through a significant drawdown? Paper conviction is different from lived conviction.

3. Do you have an emergency fund separate from your investments? A 3-6 month cash buffer means your investment portfolio can truly be long-term money.

If you can honestly say yes to those, then 100% equities is not irresponsible — it's a historically grounded decision. As you get into your 40s and 50s and start thinking about the 10-15 years before retirement, gradually shifting toward a more balanced allocation makes sense. But at 25, VTI all-in is a reasonable, well-reasoned choice.

Your parents and coworkers mean well. The advice to hold bonds is sensible as a general rule, especially for people who aren't sure how they'll handle volatility. But rules of thumb are for people who haven't thought carefully about their situation — and you clearly have.
