Your friends are right that the HSA is triple tax-advantaged — but the *most* powerful use of that advantage is often to **not** spend it on medical bills if you can avoid it.

**The key insight: there's no deadline to reimburse yourself.**

The IRS allows you to reimburse yourself from your HSA for qualified medical expenses at *any point in the future*, as long as:
1. The expense happened after your HSA was established
2. You kept the receipt/documentation

So the optimal strategy (if you can afford it) is:

1. Pay the $1,400 dental bill **out of pocket**
2. Save the receipt
3. Let the HSA money stay invested in FSKAX and compound tax-free
4. Years or decades later, pull $1,400 out of the HSA tax-free as reimbursement

If $1,400 in FSKAX grows for 20 years at ~10%/yr, it becomes ~$9,400. You reimburse yourself $1,400 then (tax-free), and the remaining gains are still in the HSA. That's a much better outcome than liquidating it today.

---

**When you *should* just use the HSA:**

- Paying $1,400 out of pocket would strain your cash flow or require credit card debt
- You're not in a particularly high tax bracket (the math is less compelling)
- You don't want the complexity of tracking receipts for years

---

**The bottom line:** Your friends aren't wrong — the HSA *can* cover this, and that's a perfectly fine use of it. But "triple tax-advantaged" actually argues for keeping that money invested *as long as possible*, not spending it now. If you have the cash, pay out of pocket and save the receipt. If you don't, use the HSA without guilt — that's what it's there for.
