Your friends are half right — the HSA *is* triple tax-advantaged, which is exactly why you should **not** use it to pay this bill.

Here's the counter-intuitive part: **there is no deadline on HSA reimbursements.** You can pay that dental bill today from your checking account, save the receipt (PDF it, store it in a folder), and reimburse yourself from the HSA in 2035, 2045, or whenever you want. The IRS doesn't care when you submit the claim, only that the expense was incurred while you were HSA-eligible.

So the real question isn't "should I use the HSA?" — it's "do I want to reimburse myself now or later?"

**If you reimburse now:** You got the deduction on contributions, but you pulled $1,400 out today instead of letting it compound tax-free for decades.

**If you pay from cash and reimburse later:** That $1,400 stays invested in FSKAX, compounds tax-free for 20–30 years, and when you eventually reimburse yourself, you get the $1,400 back *plus* you kept all the growth working for you in the meantime. At 7% for 25 years, that $1,400 grows to ~$7,600 — and the whole thing still comes out tax-free when you submit the receipt.

**The only reason to use the HSA now** is if paying $1,400 from cash flow would genuinely strain your budget or emergency fund. In that case, use it — that's better than going into debt.

Practical tip: set up a simple folder (Google Drive, Dropbox, whatever) called "HSA Receipts" and drop every medical/dental/vision receipt in there. Future you will thank present you enormously.

Your FSKAX + FXNAX setup in there is solid, by the way — low cost, broadly diversified, exactly what you want in an HSA.
