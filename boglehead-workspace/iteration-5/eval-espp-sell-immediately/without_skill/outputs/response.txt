The intuition that qualifying disposition (QD) is always better is a common misconception worth unpacking carefully.

## How QD Actually Works

QD requires **both**:
- Hold > 2 years from the **offering date** (not purchase date)
- Hold > 1 year from the **purchase date**

Since the offering period is 6 months, the purchase date clock starts 6 months after offering. So you effectively need ~18 months from purchase, not 24.

## The Tax Math — QD vs. Disqualifying Disposition (DD)

**QD on sale:**
- Ordinary income = *lesser of* (a) the actual discount received, or (b) the actual gain
- Remainder = long-term capital gain (LTCG)

**DD on sale (held >1 year from purchase):**
- Ordinary income = full spread at purchase (market price − what you paid), reported on W-2
- Remainder = LTCG

**Example: Stock went up** (offering $100 → purchase $120, sold at $150, bought at $85 via lookback)

| | Ordinary Income | LTCG | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| QD | $15 (the discount) | $50 | $65 |
| DD (LTCG) | $35 (full spread) | $30 | $65 |

QD wins here — less income taxed at ordinary rates.

**Example: Stock declined** (bought at $85, sold at $60)

| | Ordinary Income | Capital Loss |
|---|---|---|
| QD | $0 (no gain) | -$25 |
| DD | $12+ (W-2 spread, regardless) | -$32+ |

QD wins dramatically here — DD forces ordinary income even on a net loser.

## The Real Problem With Your Strategy

**You're conflating two separate decisions:**
1. Whether to *participate* in the ESPP (yes, always — it's free money)
2. Whether to *hold* the resulting shares as a long-term investment

The 15% discount + lookback gives you a guaranteed ~17.6% return on day one. That's the benefit. Holding for 2 years to save on taxes is only worth it if the stock performs well enough to justify the **concentration risk**.

**The core issue:** your salary already depends on this company. Holding significant stock on top of that means a company downturn hits you twice — income and investments simultaneously. This is the central risk Bogleheads flag with employer stock.

## What the Math Actually Implies

Say you're in the 37% ordinary + 20% LTCG brackets. On a $10K ESPP grant:

- **Sell immediately (DD, STCG):** ~$1,470 gain after tax on the discount alone
- **Hold 2 years for QD:** The tax savings vs. DD might be $200–400 on the discount portion — but you've taken on 2 years of single-stock risk to get there

The tax tail is wagging the investment dog.

## The Boglehead-Aligned Take

1. **Participate fully** — 10% contribution is almost always optimal given the discount
2. **Sell promptly** after purchase (or after 1 year from purchase for LTCG treatment on any appreciation)
3. **Reinvest proceeds in diversified index funds**
4. **Do not hold employer stock as a long-term position** because your human capital is already correlated with the company

If you've already held long enough for QD on older tranches, sell those now and diversify. For future purchases, the default should be sell-and-diversify, not hold-for-QD.
