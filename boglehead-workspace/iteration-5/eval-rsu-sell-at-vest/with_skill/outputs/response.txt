Your coworker's advice sounds reasonable on the surface but misses the key point — let me walk through it.

## The tax framing is backwards

The ordinary income tax on your RSUs was already owed the moment they vested, regardless of what you do now. That's done. The IRS treated the $10,000 as W-2 income at vest.

The question of hold vs. sell is a **purely forward-looking investment decision**, not a tax strategy. Holding for a year to get LTCG treatment saves you roughly 10–15% on any *future* gains — say $150–$300 on a 20% gain. That's real, but modest.

## The real question

After vesting, holding your RSUs is **identical to receiving $10,000 cash and choosing to buy your employer's single stock with it.**

Would you do that? If someone handed you a $10,000 check today, would you put all of it into your company's stock?

Most people wouldn't — because:
- You already have salary, bonus, career, and possibly options all tied to the same company. If the company has a bad year, your stock *and* your job are at risk simultaneously.
- Single stocks can drop 50–80% even at good companies. The LTCG tax savings you'd get from holding ~12 months won't protect you from that.
- "I believe in the company" is an available belief at any share price for any other investor — it's not an edge.

## The Boglehead answer

Sell at vest. Reinvest in a total-market index fund (VTI, VTSAX, FSKAX, etc.). Repeat each vest cycle.

If you want some company exposure because you genuinely have conviction, keep it under 5% of your net worth as a speculation allocation — not the full position.

The LTCG argument is real but small. The concentration risk compounded by employment concentration is not small. Don't let a $150–$300 potential tax savings justify carrying undiversified single-stock risk for a year.

*Not personalized financial or tax advice — for a large RSU position, a fee-only advisor or CPA can model the exact numbers for your situation.*
