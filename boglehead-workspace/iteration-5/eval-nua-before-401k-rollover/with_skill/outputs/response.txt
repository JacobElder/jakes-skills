## The numbers first

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Company stock current value | $240,000 |
| Your cost basis | $38,000 |
| **Net Unrealized Appreciation (NUA)** | **$202,000** |

That $202,000 of appreciation is the crux of everything.

## What the full rollover does to you

If you roll the company stock into a Vanguard IRA, every dollar of it becomes **ordinary income** when you withdraw it — taxed at your marginal rate (likely 22–32% in retirement). The $202,000 gain that could have been taxed at 15% capital gains rates instead becomes ordinary income forever. That opportunity is gone the moment the rollover completes.

**Rough tax difference on that $202,000 gain:**
- **NUA treatment (LTCG rate at 15%):** ~$30,300
- **IRA withdrawal (ordinary income at 25%):** ~$50,500
- **Difference: ~$20,000–$30,000 in extra taxes**, depending on your bracket

And that doesn't account for state taxes.

## What NUA actually lets you do

At separation from service (your retirement qualifies), you can take a **lump-sum distribution** of the company stock *in-kind* to a taxable brokerage account instead of rolling it. Here's what happens:

1. You pay ordinary income tax **now** on the $38,000 cost basis only — not the full $240,000
2. The shares land in your taxable brokerage
3. When you sell them (whenever you want), the $202,000 of NUA is taxed at **long-term capital gains rates** — regardless of how long you've held them since distribution

The remaining $380,000 of non-stock 401k assets roll to Vanguard IRA normally. That part of your advisor's recommendation is fine.

## What makes this a real decision vs. an obvious one

NUA isn't automatically the right call. It depends on:
- **Your current vs. retirement marginal tax rate** — if you're dropping from 32% to 12%, the ordinary income hit on the $38k basis is lower, which helps NUA math
- **When you plan to sell the shares** — if you'd hold them for years, the LTCG benefit accumulates; if you'd sell immediately, you're also paying ordinary income on the basis today
- **Your state's capital gains treatment** — some states don't distinguish LTCG from ordinary income, which reduces the NUA benefit
- **Concentration risk** — after NUA distribution you own $240k of single-company stock; plan to diversify fairly quickly

## What to do in the next 8 weeks

**Do not process any rollover paperwork** until a CPA has run the NUA numbers for your specific tax situation. This is a one-time, irreversible election. Once the shares are in an IRA, it's done — no undoing it.

Your advisor isn't wrong that a Vanguard IRA has better fund options and lower fees — that's all true. But they apparently haven't evaluated NUA, which is a significant omission for someone in your exact situation (high NUA, imminent qualifying event, 25 years of accumulated shares with low basis).

Ask your HR/plan administrator for the **exact per-share cost basis by lot** (not just the average), and bring that to a CPA or fee-only advisor who can run the actual numbers.

*This isn't personalized tax advice — given the dollar amounts and irreversibility here, a CPA review before you touch anything is essential.*
