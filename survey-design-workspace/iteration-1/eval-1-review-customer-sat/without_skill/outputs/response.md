# Survey Review: Customer Satisfaction Phone Survey

Here's a question-by-question review with the most critical issues flagged:

---

## Question 1: Speed and Friendliness (1–5 rating)

**Issue: Double-barreled question.**
Asking about "speed" and "friendliness" in the same question forces respondents to give one rating for two separate things. A customer who experienced fast but rude service has no accurate way to answer. Split this into two questions:

- *How satisfied were you with the speed of our support team's response?* (1–5)
- *How satisfied were you with the friendliness of our support team?* (1–5)

**Issue: Unlabeled numeric scale.**
Using only numbers 1–5 without anchors (e.g., "1 = Very dissatisfied, 5 = Very satisfied") is a serious problem, especially for a phone survey where respondents can't see a visual scale. Without verbal anchors, different people will interpret the direction and meaning of the scale differently, making the data unreliable. For phone administration, the interviewer must read the full anchor labels.

---

## Question 2: Pricing Fairness (Likert scale)

**Issue: Leading question.**
"Don't you agree that our pricing is fair?" presupposes the respondent should agree, which biases responses in a positive direction. Replace with a neutral framing:

- *How would you rate the fairness of our pricing?* (with Strongly agree → Strongly disagree anchors)
- Or simply: *To what extent do you agree with the following: The pricing is fair for the value received.*

The response scale options (Strongly agree through Strongly disagree) are appropriate — the problem is only in the question wording.

---

## Question 3: Channels Used (Select all that apply)

**No major issues.** The "select all that apply" format is appropriate for multi-use behavior. One minor suggestion: confirm your list is exhaustive. If customers may have used other channels (e.g., social media, in-store, a mobile app), consider adding an "Other (please specify)" option.

For phone administration, the interviewer will need to read each option aloud, which this format supports well.

---

## Question 4: Household Income (Open-ended)

**Issue: Sensitive and likely irrelevant.**
Household income is one of the most sensitive demographic questions and often results in high refusal rates. Ask yourself: Is this genuinely needed to segment or analyze your satisfaction data? If you do need it, use bracketed income ranges rather than an open field — people are significantly more willing to select a range than to state an exact figure. Also, place sensitive demographic items at the *end* of a survey (which you've done) and make them clearly optional.

Suggested format if retained:
- *What is your approximate annual household income? (Optional)*
  - Under $25,000
  - $25,000–$49,999
  - $50,000–$74,999
  - $75,000–$99,999
  - $100,000 or more
  - Prefer not to say

---

## Summary of Issues by Priority

| Priority | Issue | Question |
|---|---|---|
| High | Unlabeled scale read over the phone | Q1 |
| High | Double-barreled question | Q1 |
| High | Leading question | Q2 |
| Medium | Open-ended income field; no "prefer not to say" | Q4 |
| Low | Possibly incomplete channel list | Q3 |

Fixing Q1 and Q2 will have the biggest impact on data quality.
