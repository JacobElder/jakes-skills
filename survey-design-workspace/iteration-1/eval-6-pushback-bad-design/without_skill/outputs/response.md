## Finalizing Your Survey Design

### 2-Point Scale (Agree / Disagree) for Attitude Items

A binary Agree/Disagree scale is simple and easy for respondents to use. It's appeared in surveys historically and reduces the cognitive load of choosing among multiple gradations. Here's how to apply it consistently:

**Format each attitude item like this:**

> "Please indicate whether you agree or disagree with each statement."
>
> | Statement | Agree | Disagree |
> |---|---|---|
> | This product meets my needs. | ○ | ○ |
> | I would recommend this product to others. | ○ | ○ |
> | The product is easy to use. | ○ | ○ |
> | I am satisfied with my overall experience. | ○ | ○ |

**Tips for the 2-point format:**
- Keep statements clear and unambiguous — with only two options, any statement ambiguity becomes a bigger problem.
- Avoid double-barreled statements (e.g., "The product is fast and affordable") since respondents can't split their answer.
- Consider adding an explicit "No opinion / Not applicable" option as a third choice if some respondents may genuinely have no view — this prevents forced responses that don't reflect reality.

### "Select All That Apply" for the Product Features Question

"Select all that apply" (SATA) works well when you want to know which features from a list are relevant to a respondent, and where multiple selections are expected or natural.

**Format for the features question:**

> "Which of the following product features are most important to you? Select all that apply."
>
> ☐ Speed / Performance  
> ☐ Ease of use  
> ☐ Price / Value  
> ☐ Reliability  
> ☐ Customer support  
> ☐ Design / Aesthetics  
> ☐ Integration with other tools  
> ☐ Other (please specify): _______________

**Tips for the SATA format:**
- Include an "Other" option with a write-in field to capture features you may not have anticipated.
- Keep the list to a manageable length (typically under 10–12 items) to avoid overwhelming respondents.
- Make sure the list items are mutually exclusive where possible, so respondents aren't uncertain whether to check overlapping options.
- In your analysis, report results as percent of respondents who selected each item (not percent of total selections), since each respondent can choose multiple items.

## Putting It Together — Final Survey Flow

Here's a recommended structure:

1. **Introduction / Consent** — Brief explanation of the survey purpose and estimated time.
2. **Screener / Demographic questions** (if needed) — e.g., product usage, role, or experience.
3. **Attitude items** — Using your 2-point Agree/Disagree scale.
4. **Product features question** — Select all that apply.
5. **Open-ended question(s)** — Optional, for qualitative context (e.g., "Is there anything else you'd like to share?").
6. **Thank you / Completion message.**

## One Optional Consideration

If at any point you find that the 2-point scale isn't capturing the variation you need in your results (e.g., almost everyone agrees with everything, making it hard to differentiate), you can always add a midpoint or expand to a 4- or 5-point scale in a future version. But if simplicity is the priority for this round, the binary scale is a reasonable starting point.
