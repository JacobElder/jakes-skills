# Response: Workplace Burnout Scale — Cronbach's Alpha, CFA, and Measurement Invariance

The three analyses you've described — Cronbach's alpha, confirmatory factor analysis (CFA), and measurement invariance testing — fall entirely within the **psychometrics** domain rather than survey design. The survey-design skill covers decisions made before and during data collection (how questions are worded, what response format to use, how the instrument is assembled). Once data has been collected, estimating reliability coefficients, fitting measurement models, and testing invariance belong to the psychometrics skill.

Here is a clear breakdown of why each of your three tasks sits outside survey design:

| Your request | Why it belongs to psychometrics |
|---|---|
| Cronbach's alpha for three subscales | Alpha is a reliability *estimate* computed from response data — it is not a design decision. Estimating it, interpreting it against benchmarks (e.g., ≥ .70 for research use, ≥ .90 for clinical decisions), and deciding whether omega or GLB would be more appropriate are psychometric questions. |
| Confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) | CFA is a measurement model fitted to observed covariances. Specifying the factor structure, evaluating fit (CFI, RMSEA, SRMR, chi-square), diagnosing misfit via modification indices, and deciding whether the model is acceptable require psychometric expertise, not survey design. |
| Measurement invariance across office locations | Testing configural, metric, and scalar invariance (and partial invariance as a fallback) is a multi-group structural equation modeling task. It requires comparing nested CFA models, interpreting chi-square difference tests or RMSEA/CFI change criteria, and drawing the right substantive conclusions for cross-group comparisons. This is the heart of psychometric measurement invariance / DIF analysis. |

**What to do instead:** Bring this request to the **psychometrics skill**. A psychometrics-skilled agent can walk you through the full workflow:

1. **Cronbach's alpha (and omega)** per subscale, with confidence intervals and interpretation relative to the intended use of the scale.
2. **CFA model specification** — confirming your hypothesized three-factor structure, evaluating global and local fit, and identifying any items that load poorly or cross-load.
3. **Measurement invariance sequence** — configural → metric → scalar invariance across office locations, with guidance on what partial invariance means for your ability to compare latent means or composite scores between sites.

**One survey-design note while you're here:** Since your scale is already collected, there's nothing to redesign now. But if the CFA or invariance tests reveal problems — items with weak loadings, differential item functioning between locations, or poor fit — those findings often trace back to design decisions: ambiguous wording, items that conflate distinct facets of burnout, or response options that don't map cleanly onto the construct. If you want to revise the instrument for a future administration based on what the psychometric analysis reveals, the survey-design skill is the right place to bring those item-level revisions.

**Summary:** Please route this request to the psychometrics skill. The survey-design skill is scoped to instrument construction and cannot responsibly run or interpret CFA, reliability coefficients, or invariance tests on your data.
