# Reasoning Transcript

## Task
Stakeholder question: "Which features are *actually* important, not just which are more important than others." How do you get to absolute importance from a MaxDiff?

---

## Step 1: Identify the conceptual distinction the question requires

The question is asking about *absolute* importance, not relative importance. This is the anchoring problem. Before writing anything, the right move is to confirm whether the study was anchored.

Key principle from the skill (SKILL.md, MaxDiff section): "whenever the user mentions 'importance,' 'which features actually matter,' 'absolute,' 'genuinely valuable,' or asks whether items are *actually* important vs. merely *relatively* preferred — confirm anchoring is in the plan before going further."

And from references/maxdiff.md, Section 5: "Plain MaxDiff utilities are relative. They tell you item A > item B but not whether either is 'important' in absolute terms."

The stakeholder's question is explicitly asking for what anchoring is designed to provide.

---

## Step 2: Determine the response structure

The response needs to cover:
1. Why unanchored MaxDiff cannot answer the question (not a failure of the method — it's just a different question)
2. What anchoring provides and how the two methods work (direct binary anchor vs. dual-response anchor)
3. What to do if the study was NOT anchored (honest answer: can't salvage; re-field)
4. How to report anchored results in a way that matches the stakeholder's actual question
5. Cross-wave considerations (only anchored share-above-anchor is comparable across studies)

---

## Step 3: Key content decisions

**Do not hedge about "importance" being a spectrum.** The skill's guidance is direct: unanchored utilities cannot support absolute claims. The response should be equally direct.

**Lead with the conceptual gap, not method mechanics.** The stakeholder doesn't need to understand the logit normalization; they need to understand why the current data may or may not answer their question.

**Distinguish the two anchoring methods but give a clear default.** The reference says direct binary anchor is the recommended default for most studies. The response should give a clear recommendation, not present two equally-weighted options.

**The re-fielding recommendation must be present if anchoring was absent.** The skill is explicit: "there is no post-hoc fix." Softening this would be misleading.

**Report structure matters.** The skill notes that stakeholders read point estimates as truth and that leading with utilities alone invites misinterpretation. The response explicitly prescribes leading with share-above-anchor.

---

## Step 4: What the response deliberately does NOT include

- Does not recommend post-hoc imputation of a threshold from utilities (invalid)
- Does not recommend comparing anchored shares to unanchored scores from prior waves (invalid)
- Does not suggest that a high relative utility is equivalent to high absolute importance (the core trap)
- Does not suggest the stakeholder's question is answerable without knowing whether anchoring was included
- Does not repeat basic MaxDiff mechanics (the skill explicitly says the platform documentation covers that and to avoid repeating it)

---

## Final check

The skill's anchoring trigger is explicit: the question contains "actually important" and "important vs. relatively preferred" — these are direct trigger phrases. The response correctly centers anchoring as the answer, explains both methods, gives the default recommendation (direct binary), explains what to do without anchoring, and gives a concrete reporting structure for the anchored case.

The response avoids claiming anchoring solves a poorly-designed study or that absolute importance thresholds can be recovered without it.
